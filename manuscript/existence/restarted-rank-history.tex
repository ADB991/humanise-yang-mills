\subsection{A fixed-coupling history for the rank comparison}
\label{sec:restarted-rank-history}

The rank tests compare the interacting fields with a free field over a
short interval of scales. They require an auxiliary integration history.
The coupling is held fixed on that interval, and the local additive
remainder is controlled in value. We give the density construction and
its bounds here. The calibrated history constructed earlier supplies the
endpoint and the kernels; it is still the history defining the fields.

\subsubsection{The additive remainder and its exact density factor}

On a finite torus $\Lambda$, write a representation's density as
\[
 R(U)=\exp\{v|\Lambda|-A(U)-E(U)\}\,G(U),
 \qquad E(U)=\sum_\alpha E_\alpha(U).
\]
Here $A$ is its path action, $G$ its forced gas with the prescribed
supports, inventories and eligibility tests, and $E_\alpha$ are real,
measurable, gauge-invariant local functions: their values agree when the local links and the relevant final flag data agree. Each $E_\alpha$ vanishes
unless its support $X_\alpha$ is healthy at the representation's threshold.
Give $X_\alpha$ an anchor $a_\alpha\in X_\alpha$. Suppose $b_\alpha\ge0$
majorizes $|E_\alpha|$ and, for the representation's support weight
$M(X)\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-anchored}
 \sum_{\alpha:a_\alpha=x}M(X_\alpha)b_\alpha\le q
 \quad(x\in\Lambda).
\end{equation}
The sums in this inequality are assumed summable. Since the anchor set
is finite, summing it gives
\[
 \sum_\alpha |E_\alpha(U)|\le |\Lambda|q.
\]
Thus the sum defining $E$ converges absolutely, is measurable and gauge
invariant, and $e^{-E}$ has the finite-volume bound $e^{|\Lambda|q}$.
Let $R^0$ be obtained by deleting just the additive terms. Its paths,
coupling, scalar, forced activities and their tests are unchanged. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-white}
 R=e^{-E}R^0.
\end{equation}
For any finite family of source variables, the same identity holds for
its polynomial-valued density. Consequently the integrability of that
density follows from the corresponding envelope for $R^0$. The estimate
$e^{|\Lambda|q}$ is used only for finite-volume integrability; it is not
used as a uniform bound in the volume limit.

\subsubsection{A local multiplier keeps the expansion on the actual density}

Here is the local operation behind the construction. For a masked
additive term $E_\alpha$, insert the letter
\[
 h_\alpha=e^{-E_\alpha}-1.
\]
The identity $\prod_\alpha(1+h_\alpha)=e^{-E}$ is exact. Its infinite
version follows from absolute summability, since
$|e^{-t}-1|\le e^{|t|}|t|$ and $\sum_\alpha b_\alpha<\infty$.
If the weighted footprint estimate for these letters has allowance
$\omega$, attach them to the same component alphabet as the fluctuation
integral. The component estimate of
Subsection~\ref{sec:mixed-activity-prices} and the reassembly identity of
Subsection~\ref{sec:gaussian-reassembly} apply with this enlarged alphabet.
The old forced labels and their eligibility conditions are retained.
In particular the resulting identity integrates $e^{-E}R^0=R$.
This construction cannot be replaced by integrating $R^0$ alone.

For later use, denote by $\delta$ the weighted allowance of the
unmarked coarse letters, including the multiplier letters. The
regular-gas estimate of Subsection~\ref{sec:forest-average-log}, at the
purchased support exponent, supplies a masked logarithmic family
$\ell_\gamma$ with anchored cost at most $16\delta$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-log}
 G_{\rm reg}(U)=\exp\{-\sum_\gamma\ell_\gamma(U)\}.
\end{equation}
The elementary numerical conditions used at this step include
\[
 16\delta\le1,\qquad
 e^{2\delta}-1\le1-e^{-16\delta},\qquad
 16\delta\,\mathcal R(B)\le1.
\]
Here $\mathcal R(B)$ is the connected-support counting constant in the
regular logarithm bound, and $B$ is the coarse support radius. They
are imposed on the same letter prices, not on a different auxiliary
normalization. If the integrand has holes around marked sites, the
forced-exclusion identity gives the corresponding localized logarithms
with those holes. The scalar identity without holes and these identities
with holes therefore have the same normalization.

Choose the new coarse scalar from the positive fluctuation prefactor.
If the prefactor is $C>0$, its contribution to the scalar per coarse
site is $|\Lambda_c|^{-1}\log C$. Positivity follows from the positive
coupling, the positive chart ratio, and the positive determinant of the
flat precision. In particular this logarithm does not choose a branch.
Let $A_c$ be the retained coarse path action and $\beta B_c$ the
reference action used in the fluctuation formula. Its local correction
is $\beta B_c-A_c$. Add its masked local pieces to
$\ell_\gamma$. If the former have anchored cost $\epsilon$, the
combined family has cost
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-newcost}
                         \epsilon+16\delta.
\end{equation}
Indeed the two nonnegative majorant sums add at every anchor. The
identity for its exponential is exactly
\[
 e^{v_c|\Lambda_c|-\beta B_c}\,G_{\rm reg}
       =e^{v_c|\Lambda_c|-A_c-E_c}.
\]
Choose the coarse forced activities by the addresswise construction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:corrected-forced-output}: attach the hole and
boundary factors to the unmarked forced components, sum at each full
support and flag inventory, and store those sums as the output's
archived activities. The regular/forced decomposition of the same expansion then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-push}
                  R_c=\mathcal K_\beta R_f
\end{equation}
on every coarse configuration, including configurations with flags.
To check this last assertion, first keep the source polynomial in the
finite expansion. Its coefficient of the empty inventory equals the
unmarked scalar density. The reassembly and exclusion identities hold
coefficientwise and preserve exact flag coverage. Setting the source
family empty therefore identifies their scalar coefficients on every
configuration. There is no division by a density or by a forced
activity in this argument.

\subsubsection{Symmetrizing the additive family}

The newly obtained sum $E_c$ is invariant under signed lattice motions,
but an individual choice of anchored pieces need not have a symmetric
indexing. Let $H$ be the finite group of these motions on the torus.
Replace a piece $f_\alpha$ by the family
\[
 |H|^{-1}f_\alpha(gU),\qquad (g,\alpha)\in H\times\mathcal I,
\]
with the correspondingly transported support and anchor. For fixed $g$,
the anchored bound is the old bound at the inverse image of the anchor.
Multiplication by $|H|^{-1}$ and summation over $g$ preserve that bound.
Absolute summability permits interchange of the finite group sum and
the piece sum. Since the total was invariant, the new total is
$|H|^{-1}\sum_gE_c(gU)=E_c(U)$. Left multiplication in $H$ permutes the
new indices, giving the required covariance of individual addresses.
Reindex this countable family and, if necessary, add zero terms.
The gas and all forced addresses are unchanged. This produces the
locality, measurability and motion properties together with
\eqref{eq:restart-push}.

\subsubsection{The cost over a short interval}

Fix a block ratio $L>1$ and a width parameter $b\ge0$. For large $s$ put
\[
 y=\log\log s,\quad A_s=e^{y^3},\quad
 q_n=s^{-1/200}A_s^{4n},\quad
 \omega_n=q_nA_s,\quad \delta_n=q_nA_s^3.
\]
The two parts of the comparison interval have lengths $h_1,h_2$.
Their definitions are the ceilings
\[
 h_1=\left\lceil\frac{(b+2)y^2}{\log L}\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 h_2=\left\lceil\frac{\log(M_s^{10})}{\log L}\right\rceil,
 \qquad h=h_1+h_2,
\]
where $M_s=\lceil(\log s)^2\rceil$; the support wall is $M_s^2$.
The support-radius estimates give $\log(M_s^{10})\le y^2$ eventually.
Thus $h\le C_{L,b}y^2+2\le y^4$ eventually.
For every fixed integer $r\ge0$, uniformly for $0\le n\le h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-decay}
 q_nA_s^r\le s^{-1/400}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $s$. In fact its logarithm is at most
$-(\log s)/200+(4y^4+r)y^3$. For $y\ge1$ the positive term is at most
$(4+r)y^7$. Since $y^7e^{-y}\to0$ and $\log s=e^y$, it is at most
$(\log s)/400$ eventually, proving the claim.

The footprint price of an additive input with cost $q_n$ is at most
\[
 C_{L,a,t}(42B+1)^4q_n,
\]
where the support reserve $t$ is strictly positive, $a$ is the fixed
support exponent, and $B\le M_s^2$. All factors except the displayed
radius and $q_n$ are fixed before $s$ is increased. The wall estimates
imply $C_{L,a,t}(42M_s^2+1)^4\le A_s$ eventually. Hence the multiplier
letters cost at most $\omega_n$. The remaining unmarked letters have
their purchased local bounds; their combined logarithm is allowed cost
$16\delta_n$. The path correction costs at most $s^{-1/200}$. These
bounds close the recurrence because, as soon as $A_s\ge17$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-recurrence}
 s^{-1/200}+16\delta_n\le q_{n+1}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $s^{-1/200}\le q_n\le\delta_n$ and
$q_{n+1}=A_s\delta_n$.

For clarity, the small exponential conditions also hold uniformly
through this interval. If $0\le t\le1$, then $e^t-1\le2t$.
Consequently $e^{2\delta_n}-1\le4\delta_n$. If
$0\le\delta_n\le1/16$, the inequalities
$e^{16\delta_n}\ge1+16\delta_n$ and
$(1+16\delta_n)^{-1}\le1-4\delta_n$ give
\[
 e^{2\delta_n}-1\le1-e^{-16\delta_n}.
\]
The second rational inequality follows after multiplying by its positive
denominator: $12\delta_n-64\delta_n^2\ge0$.
The factors $\mathcal R(B)$, the insertion inflation and any fixed
comparison polynomial have at most the purchased powers of
$e^{y^2}$; multiplying \eqref{eq:restart-decay} by such factors still
gives zero in the limit. The forced-gas allowance is smaller than every
negative power of $s$, uniformly for the local scale parameter in
$[s/2,s]$. It therefore pays the support-counting factors in the
normalization-with-holes estimates. All these are eventual conditions
after $L,b$ and the finite menu have been fixed.

\subsubsection{Re-expressing the starting density}
\label{subsec:restart-endpoint-construction}

We first supply the stronger forced allowance at the starting level.
This is a change of presentation on one torus, before applying a
blocking kernel. Write $c_{\rm lin}$ and $c_{\rm dir}$ for the two
positive coefficients of its clip; the clip condition includes
$c_{\rm lin}<c_{\rm dir}/16$. Fix a positive path rate $\sigma$, a
path coefficient bound $C$, and a support exponent $a>0$. All these
parameters are fixed before the scale $s$ tends to infinity. In particular,
each quadratic path row has
$\sum_j|c_j|e^{\sigma\,\mathrm{length}_j}\le C$; each cubic and
quartic atom family has the corresponding absolute true-length
coefficient sum at most $C$. The loop family has that bound with
$\Gamma_s$ in place of $C$. The forced gas uses its full support and
inventory addresses with the original eligibility and exclusion tests;
its stated allowance is an absolute weighted site-sum bound.
Put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 H_s=\exp((\log\log s)^2),\quad
 h_s=\exp((\log\log s)^{3/2}),\\
 t=s^{-1/2}H_s,\quad \tau=s^{-1/2}h_s,
 \quad M=\lceil(\log s)^2\rceil,\quad B=M^2.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The old budget has radius $M$ and threshold $t$; the new one has
radius $B$ and threshold $\tau$. Their support exponent and derivative
order are unchanged. Denote the corresponding masked additive sums by
$E_t,E_\tau$ and path actions by $A_t,A_\tau$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact endpoint re-expression]
\label{prop:restart-endpoint}
Suppose the original endpoint has coupling $s/2\le\beta\le2s$, the
fixed path and clip bounds above, quadratic-loop coefficient bound
$\Gamma_s\le2H_s$, additive allowance $s^{-11/10}$, and forced allowance
$w_s=\exp(-H_s^{1/5})$. Fix
$0<a_0\le c_{\rm lin}/20$. For all sufficiently large $s$, uniformly
in the period and endpoint field, there is a presentation of exactly
the same density with the same path, clip, coupling and scalar, budget
$(B,\tau)$, additive allowance at most $s^{-11/10}$, and forced
allowance
\[
                         G(s)=e^{-a_0h_s^2}.
\]
It retains the original additive coefficients with their new masks.
In particular its additive value allowance is at most $q_0=s^{-1/200}$,
and deleting those coefficients gives the neutral incoming law needed
by the local compiler.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We construct all factors before grouping them. A flagged site at
threshold $\tau$ is a site touched by a plaquette whose chord exceeds
$\tau$. Write $F_\tau(U)$ for this set. Eventually $2\tau\le t$.
For a plaquette chord $r$ put
\[
 d_t(r)=\begin{cases}c_{\rm dir}r^2/2,&r\le t,\\
                     c_{\rm lin}tr,&r>t,\end{cases}
 \qquad \Delta(r)=d_t(r)-d_\tau(r).
\]
Then $\Delta\ge0$, and $\Delta(r)\ge c_{\rm lin}\tau^2$ for
$r>\tau$. On $\tau<r\le t$ this follows from
$\Delta(r)=c_{\rm dir}r^2/2-c_{\rm lin}\tau r
\ge(c_{\rm dir}/2-c_{\rm lin})r^2\ge c_{\rm lin}\tau^2$;
on $r>t$ use $t-\tau\ge\tau$. Distribute $\Delta(r)$ equally among the sites
touched by its plaquette. There are at most five such sites, including
on a wrapping torus. The resulting local charges $d_x$ satisfy
\[
 \sum_{x\in F_\tau}d_x=\sum_{\text{plaquettes }p}\Delta(r_p),
 \qquad d_x\ge c_{\rm lin}\tau^2/5\quad(x\in F_\tau).
\]
A plaquette with nonzero charge has all its touched sites in $F_\tau$,
which proves the equality without discarding charges at healthy sites.

The other quadratic contribution to $A_t-A_\tau$ is the squared
path row on the event that the old kept test holds and the new one
fails. Truncate the row at true path length $B$, before squaring,
and call its resulting nonnegative half-square $b_x$ at anchor $x$,
including that event indicator and the signed-channel average.
The kept test at threshold $\tau$ reads only a radius-$B$ neighborhood
once $2\le\tau\exp(B/c_{\rm relax})$: beyond that neighborhood the
chord bound two makes its test automatic. Thus $b_x$ reads at most
a radius-$2B$ neighborhood, and vanishes if the radius-$B$
neighborhood is $\tau$-healthy. Distribute $b_x$ equally among the
sites of $F_\tau$ in that neighborhood. If there are none, $b_x=0$
and no division is made. Summing these shares gives nonnegative
charges $b'_z$, with $\sum_zb'_z=\sum_xb_x$, supported within radius
$4B$ of $z$. Perform the same operation for each signed channel with
its original averaging weight.

Set $u=a_0h_s^2$ and, at a flagged site, set
\[
 X_z(U)=\exp[-\beta(d_z(U)+b'_z(U))];
\]
set $X_z=0$ when the site is unflagged. Since
$\beta\tau^2\ge h_s^2/2$ and $a_0\le c_{\rm lin}/20$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-endpoint-signal}
 |X_z|\le e^{-2u},\qquad
 \prod_{z\in F_\tau}X_z
       =\exp[-\beta\sum_z(d_z+b'_z)].
\end{equation}
In particular each selected site factor supplies one factor $e^{-u}$
which can be reserved for its connected component.

Here are the remaining exponent changes and their prices. Write
\[
 D=-(A_t-A_\tau)+\beta\sum_z(d_z+b'_z)+(E_\tau-E_t).
\]
This is the sum of the omitted quadratic row pairs, the differences
of the cubic, quartic and loop masks, and the difference of the
additive masks. Each summand has a nonempty full read support and
vanishes when that support is $\tau$-healthy. Their absolute site
price at any fixed enlarged exponent $a+2$ is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-endpoint-error}
 \epsilon_s\le C_1B^{C_2}
 \left[\beta C^2e^{-\sigma B/4}
       +\Gamma_st^2+\beta C(t^3+t^4)+s^{-11/10}\right]
                         =s^{-1/2+o(1)}.
\end{equation}
The constants depend only on the fixed profile and exponent. To verify
this bound, expand the difference of a full square and its truncated
square as ordered pairs of path labels, with at least one length
exceeding $B$. Extract $e^{-\sigma B/4}$ from the two absolute
exponential row sums after paying their full path supports, as in
\eqref{eq:inherited-tail-budget}. The kept-test support adds a
radius-$2B$ halo, whose cover cost is fixed. The difference of two
nested masks has absolute value at most the old masked atom bound;
for an order-$j$ atom this is its coefficient times $t^j$.
The full path support has $B$-cover at most $1+2j+\mathrm{length}/B$,
so $(a+2)/B\le\sigma/2$ eventually leaves a positive length reserve.
For an old additive support $Y$, the connected-cover construction gives
\[
 c_B(\operatorname{halo}_8Y)
       \le C_3+C_3(M/B)c_M(Y).
\]
Take $s$ large enough that $C_3(a+3)M/B\le a$. This converts its
old weighted bound to the new one. Converting anchors to site hits
costs a polynomial in $B$, by the shell sum in
Subsection~\ref{sec:inherited-activity-prices}, with the extra unit of
cover exponent reserved for this conversion. The same calculation
prices the halo-enlarged old forced labels by
$C_1B^{C_2}w_s$. These arguments retain all label multiplicities and
true path lengths. Finally $H_s=s^{o(1)}$, giving the last equality
in \eqref{eq:restart-endpoint-error}.

Coalesce the summands of $D$ with the same full support, giving $D_Y$.
There are finitely many supports on this torus, and the coalescing is
absolutely convergent by the site bound. For large $s$, each
$|D_Y|\le\epsilon_s\le1/4$. Put $W_Y=e^{D_Y}-1$. Then
\[
 e^D=\prod_Y(1+W_Y),\qquad |W_Y|\le2|D_Y|,
\]
and $W_Y=0$ on a $\tau$-healthy support. Combining with
\eqref{eq:restart-endpoint-signal} gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-endpoint-factor}
 e^{-A_t-E_t}=e^{-A_\tau-E_\tau}
                   \prod_Y(1+W_Y)\prod_{z\in F_\tau}X_z.
\end{equation}

Multiply by the original forced gas and expand the optional factors.
Enlarge old forced supports by eight sites to read their original
eligibility tests; retain those tests and their original mutual
exclusions inside every term. Form connected components using
intersections of these enlarged supports, the $Y$'s and the
radius-$4B$ supports of $X_z$. Every nonzero component contains a
selected $X_z$: a nonzero $W_Y$ meets a new flag, and an old forced
label contains an old flag, which is also a new flag. The factor for
that flag has intersecting support and is present in the product.
Record as the new inventory exactly the selected flag sites in each
component, requiring it to equal $F_\tau$ on that component's support.
Require also that the union of old inventories is exactly the set of
old flags on this component's support, and retain each old label's
eligibility test. The components then
have disjoint supports and cover all new flags. Conversely their
constituents recover the original compatible forced collection and
optional subset uniquely. This proves equality of the two gas sums, first for finite label
families. The old absolute site bound, the finite-volume sum of the
optional prices, and the connected majorants below permit passage to
countable families by absolute convergence.

After reserving $e^{-u}$ per selected site factor, the combined
weighted site price is at most
\[
 \rho_s=2\epsilon_s+C_1B^{C_2}w_s
                 +(8B+1)^4e^{(a+1)9^4}e^{-u}.
\]
Indeed a radius-$4B$ ball has $B$-cover at most $9^4$, and at most
$(8B+1)^4$ of these balls contain a given site. Here
$\rho_s=s^{-1/2+o(1)}$, so $(2B+1)^4\rho_s\le1$ and
$e^{\rho_s}-1\le1$ eventually. The rooted connected-set estimate
proved in Subsection~\ref{sec:actual-mixed-marked-compiler}, using one
unit of support reserve, bounds the resulting forced site sum by
$e^{-u}(e^{\rho_s}-1)\le e^{-u}$. No enumeration of possible flag
subsets costs an additional factor: the selected site labels already
specify the inventory, and its equality test only removes terms.
Sum the resulting majorants and values at each full support and
inventory address to obtain the new forced activities.

All constructions preserve locality, measurability and gauge symmetry.
Signed motions permute their labels; averaging the original channel
convention, or reindexing each full orbit, preserves the bounds.
The old regular coefficients need no replacement. Their derivative
bounds restrict to the smaller healthy domain and smaller allowed
coordinate directions, while increasing the cover radius decreases
its weight. They therefore retain their old additive allowance.
Equation~\ref{eq:restart-endpoint-factor} and the gas bijection prove
exact equality of densities with unchanged scalar and coupling.
Deleting the additive coefficients leaves the same path and forced
bounds and zero additive cost, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Joining to the endpoint}

Choose a comparison depth $k$ and cutoff $N\ge k$. Above $k$, retain
the original history. At $k$, use its actual representation in the
chosen local presentation. Below $k$, set the coupling identically
$\beta_{N,p,k}$ and apply the preceding construction down to the lower
edge of the comparison interval. The local scale parameter lies in
$[s/2,s]$, with $s$ the clock coupling at $k$, and the endpoint bound
$|\beta_{N,p,k}-s|\le s/8$ places this constant coupling in the
purchased band $x/2\le\beta_{N,p,k}\le3x$ throughout the interval:
$7s/8\ge x/2$ and $9s/8\le3x$ for $s/2\le x\le s$.

Use Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-endpoint} for the presentation at $k$,
with $a_0=a_{\rm coast}$. Its fixed-profile restrictions are included in
the local purchases; for a finite adjacent-profile menu take the smaller
positive admissible $a_0$. The endpoint has exactly the original density,
the prescribed path and clip, additive cost at most $q_0$, and forced
allowance $G(s)$. It is this re-expressed endpoint that starts the
induction.

Inductively, the input at a step has cost $q_n$, the output has cost
at most $q_{n+1}$ by \eqref{eq:restart-recurrence}, and their unnormalized
densities satisfy \eqref{eq:restart-push} at that very same coupling.
Only the new entry is installed at each backward step. Thus all entries
already constructed, including the endpoint and its marked labels, are
retained. Composition proves the density identities for the entire
auxiliary history. The comparison with one or two marked sites uses the
same component alphabet and the same hole normalization. Its leading
and remainder estimates are separate from this unmarked construction;
they must be verified on these actual densities before the rank tests
can be applied.

\subsubsection{Steps across a stage boundary}
\label{subsec:restart-stage-boundary}

The cost estimate above fixes one block ratio $L$. A comparison window may
contain levels of two consecutive stages. We state what a step whose fine
and coarse levels lie in different stages has to be supplied with, and
check that the numerical part of the construction then passes across the
boundary.

Consider the step from the fine level $m+1$ to the coarse level $m$, at the
constant coupling $\beta=\beta_{N,p,k}$, with stage pair
$(J(m+1),J(m))$ and input cost $q_n$. It is the restarted step of this
section: it uses the fixed coupling $\beta$ and the new multiplier letters
$h_\alpha$, and its fine and coarse clips, budget radius and support
radius $B\le M_s^2$ are those of the two levels at that pair. We use the
following three hypotheses for that step.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(V1)] \emph{Component bounds.} The unmarked coarse letters of the
 step other than the multiplier letters, built at the coupling $\beta$
 with the clips, radii and support exponent $a$ of the pair, have weighted
 allowance at most $\delta_n-\omega_n$ at the support weight. The
 component estimate of Subsection~\ref{sec:mixed-activity-prices} applies to
 the alphabet enlarged by the multiplier letters.
\item[(V2)] \emph{Path correction.} With $A_c$ the retained coarse path
 action of the coarse clip of the pair and $\beta B_c$ the reference
 action of the fluctuation formula at the coupling $\beta$, the masked
 local pieces of $\beta B_c-A_c$ have anchored cost at most $s^{-1/200}$.
\item[(V3)] \emph{Reassembly.} The reassembly identity of
 Subsection~\ref{sec:gaussian-reassembly} and the forced-exclusion identity
 hold for that enlarged alphabet, with exact flag coverage and the
 addresswise coarse forced activities of
 Proposition~\ref{prop:corrected-forced-output}.
\end{enumerate}
At a fixed ratio $L$ these are the bounds used in the preceding
subsections. Given (V1)--(V3), the output of the step is described by data
of the same kind at the coarse level: a path action, a forced gas with its
tests, an additive family of anchored cost at most $q_{n+1}$, and the push
identity \eqref{eq:restart-push}. That output does not refer to the stage
of the fine level.

\begin{lemma}[Stage changes inside the interval]
\label{lem:restart-stage-boundary}
Let the interval of the construction contain levels of the stages $r$ and
$r-1$ only. Suppose that (V1)--(V3) hold at every step of the interval,
each for its own stage pair, which is one of $(r,r)$, $(r,r-1)$ and
$(r-1,r-1)$. Suppose also that the thresholds after which the eventual
inequalities of the cost estimate hold are taken as the larger of those
for $L_r$ and $L_{r-1}$. Then the inductive joining argument above holds
on the whole interval, with the same costs $q_n$ and the same density
identities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The induction hypothesis at a step is an input of cost $q_n$ with its
density. The multiplier letters cost at most $\omega_n$ by the footprint
bound, in which the block ratio enters only through $C_{L,a,t}$; take the
larger of the two constants. By (V1) the unmarked allowance is at most
$\delta_n$, so the regular logarithm has cost at most $16\delta_n$. By (V2)
and \eqref{eq:restart-newcost} the combined family has cost at most
$s^{-1/200}+16\delta_n$, which is at most $q_{n+1}$ by
\eqref{eq:restart-recurrence}. By (V3) the push identity
\eqref{eq:restart-push} holds at that step. In $h_1,h_2$ take $L$ to be the
smaller of $L_{r-1},L_r$. Then \eqref{eq:restart-decay} and
\eqref{eq:restart-recurrence} hold for every step beyond the later of the
two thresholds, and the rest of the argument is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Purchasing the actual adjacent steps}
\label{subsec:restart-actual-purchases}

We now verify (V1)--(V3) for the local construction, rather than
assuming them for a restarted history. The fixed input is the funded
chart/stamp contract: the original path bounds and binding, the actual
adjacent tubes and their metric floors, the room and chart parameters,
and the local reserve choices of
Subsection~\ref{sec:actual-local-compiler-handoff}. The support purchases
are made at the larger raw exponent and its reserve in
Proposition~\ref{prop:allarity-compiler-reserve}. These are purchases
on each stage pair, before the coupling and before the period; they do
not assume any estimates on the restarted representations. As in the
all-stage selection, a statement for a fixed pair below is made into a
request at that pair, and the finite set of requests at a stage is
intersected before choosing its ready time. No supremum of constants
over infinitely many profiles is taken.

First record the path correction explicitly. On a coarse level with
threshold $\tau_x=x^{-1/2}\mathrm{hot}(x)$, support exponent $a$ and
radius $B_c$, let $C_c$ bound each of its cubic and quartic coefficient
sums at path rate $\sigma_c>0$, and let $\Gamma_c$ bound its quadratic
loop sum. If $a/B_c\le\sigma_c$, the anchored price of the correction
$\beta B_c^{\rm ref}-A_c$ is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-path-price}
 \beta e^{7a}C_c\tau_x^3+
 \beta e^{9a}C_c\tau_x^4+e^{5a}\Gamma_c\tau_x^2.
\end{equation}
Here $B_c^{\rm ref}$ denotes the reference base action, not the budget
radius. Indeed its difference from the full path action consists of
minus the cubic and quartic terms with coefficient $\beta$, and minus
the loop terms with coefficient one, with their original healthy masks.
An order-$j$ atom of true length $d$ has cover at most $1+2j+d/B_c$.
Its weighted value is therefore bounded by
$e^{a(1+2j)}\tau_x^j$ times its coefficient weighted by
$e^{\sigma_cd}$. Sum these coefficient bounds at each anchor, and
average the signed channels with their probability weights. This proves
\eqref{eq:restart-path-price}, including all winding paths.
Since $\Gamma_c\le2\exp((\log\log x)^2)$ and $\beta\le4x$, its
right side is $x^{-1/2+o(1)}$. Also $a/B_c\le\sigma_c$ eventually.
Uniformly for $x\in[s/2,s]$, this proves (V2), with bound $s^{-1/200}$.

\begin{proposition}[Closure of the restarted local construction]
\label{prop:restart-actual-closure}
With the funded chart/stamp data just specified and the original
endpoint bounds of Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-endpoint}, the
restarted representations can be constructed throughout the comparison
interval. At level $i$, their path and clip are those of the original
level, their coupling is $\beta_{N,p,k}$, their additive value cost is
at most $q_{k-i}$, and their forced allowance is
$G(x_i)=\exp(-a_{\rm coast}\mathrm{hot}(x_i)^2)$, where
$x_i=\widehat\beta_i$. Their neutral versions have the inherited law
required by the actual local compiler. On every adjacent step the
construction satisfies (V1)--(V3) on its own corrected alphabet and
has the exact density identity \eqref{eq:restart-push}. The upper and
lower compiler estimates use this same alphabet and normalization.
All thresholds are uniform in cutoff, period, fields and marked
families with the fixed arity cap and price parameters, with the
fixed-profile dependence allowed by the funded contract.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Matching the geometric data.}
The clock estimate in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ex-lattice-firesky-clock-fields} gives more than its
stated half-band. Its upper bound for the total logarithmic scale loss
is $o(s_k)$, so, increasing the ready threshold, one has
$3s/4\le x_i\le s$ on the whole interval. Since
$|\beta_{N,p,k}-s|\le s/8$, this gives
\[
                 x_i/2\le\beta_{N,p,k}\le2x_i.
\]
The clock relation on a step is exactly
$x_{i+1}=\beta_F(x_i)$ for its own fine block size. Retiming gives
the coarse threshold $\operatorname{temp}(r(x_i))$, while the fine
threshold is $x_{i+1}^{-1/2}\mathrm{hot}(x_{i+1})$. The logarithmic
radii have the required comparison $B_f\le2B_c$ eventually, and the
budget wall dominates the fixed multiples of the local radius.
These are the local radius and band conditions of the corrected
compiler. Its remaining room and chart inequalities are the requests
at the actual stage pair in the funded contract.

Retain the two original beds and the original tube at this step.
Their path attachment, diagonal identity, tower roll, metric floors
and path bounds are properties of those paths and clips, not of the
additive or forced coefficients. Consequently the alongside certificate
is unchanged when those coefficients are replaced. The induction below
always retains the prescribed path and clip, so the upward path and
high clip of this actual tube are exactly those of its incoming
representation. This verifies the same-data requirement of the local
handoff, including at a stage boundary; equality of the two stage
indices is not used.

\emph{The incoming law.}
Start with Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-endpoint}. Inductively suppose
the stated bounds hold at level $i+1$. Delete only its additive family.
The forced activities, their eligibility tests, path and clip remain
unchanged, and the additive cost is zero. This gives the neutral law
with any nonnegative additive allowance, in particular the calendar
allowance used by the inherited estimate. Its loop coefficient bound
is the original $\Gamma_{i+1}\le2H_{x_{i+1}}$, which is eventually
smaller than the required positive power of the local fine parameter.
The genuine representation separately has value cost $q_n$, where
$n=k-(i+1)$. There is no assertion that $q_n$ meets the much smaller
calendar derivative allowance. The correction letters use only its
value bound and their full local supports.

\emph{Prices on this alphabet.}
Apply the actual local handoff to this tuple. Its matrix and both word
inventories, owner bound, and all-matched warning estimate are uniform
in the incoming field and store. In particular
Lemma~\ref{lem:actual-neutral-warning} prices a product of warning
factors and genuine remainder factors in the same Gaussian integral.
Let $m$ be the larger of $G(x_i)^{-2}$ and $G(x_{i+1})^{-1}$, and let
$l$ be the larger unforced multiplier needed by the two compiler uses.
The latter has $\log l=O((\log\log s)^2)$ by the upper calculation
($l\le\max(1,H_s^{q-3})$); the lower use has multiplier one.
Fix these before introducing any marked family.

We verify the inflated body-product premise, including its site bound.
Each forced body component contains an owner. Charge its factor $m$
to such an owner, bounding it by $m$ for each owner if there are several.
For unforced components charge $l$ to a constituent; bounding by $l$
for every constituent only increases the price. An owner of $r$ seed
shares is bounded by $\exp[-K\mathrm{hot}(x_{i+1})^2r]$ after its
normalization is paid, with the positive margin $K$ of the local
handoff. Uniformly for $x_i,x_{i+1}\in[3s/4,s]$,
\[
 \frac{\mathrm{hot}(x_i)^2}{\mathrm{hot}(x_{i+1})^2}\longrightarrow1.
\]
To check this, the difference of their $\log\log$ arguments is
$O(1/\log s)$; the mean value theorem applied to $2t^{3/2}$
bounds the difference of the logarithms by
$O(\sqrt{\log\log s}/\log s)$. Hence $\log m\le
4a_{\rm coast}\mathrm{hot}(x_{i+1})^2$ eventually. The purchased
inequality $2a_{\rm coast}\le K/4$ leaves at least half the owner
suppression. The factor $l$, the finite decoration costs and the
packet count $H_BD_B^{2r}$ from
Subsection~\ref{sec:mixed-activity-prices} are absorbed in the remaining
margin: their logarithms per seed are $O((\log\log s)^2+\log B)$,
whereas $\mathrm{hot}(x_{i+1})^2$ dominates every fixed multiple of
these quantities. Thus the inflated owner site price is smaller than
any fixed negative power of $s$. The joint warning price has the same
property, even after the fixed halo counts and $l$ are paid, by
\eqref{eq:warning-price}. Its exponent grows like a positive constant
times $\exp(\tfrac12(\log\log s)^2)$, which dominates $\log s$.

The remaining neutral auxiliary letters have site price at most
$r(x_i)^{-1/48}$ by the corrected exponent decomposition. Multiplication
by $l$ and fixed cover/counting costs changes this to
$s^{-1/48+o(1)}$. The projected old forced background has price at
most $\mathcal W(B_c)L^4T_EG(x_{i+1})$, by
\eqref{eq:mixed-cover-charge} and its site version. This too is smaller
than any fixed negative power of $s$. The background need not itself
pay $m$: exact inventory coverage places every forced background
inventory in a body owner component, which supplies that inflation,
as in the proof of \eqref{eq:mixed-club-integral}.

Finally, the genuine additive letters have uninflated footprint price
at most $C(42B_c+1)^4q_n$. All fixed powers of $B_c$ and all factors
$l$ are $\exp(O((\log\log s)^2))$. Since
$A_s=\exp((\log\log s)^3)$, each fixed product of these factors is
at most $A_s^{1/4}$ eventually. In particular the inflated additive
site price is at most $q_nA_s^{1/4}$. There are finitely many such
products in this argument; choose their thresholds together, uniformly
for $n\le h$. The incoming bound \eqref{eq:restart-decay} makes the
combined site price times every fixed radius polynomial tend to zero.
The rooted-tree estimate of
Subsection~\ref{sec:actual-mixed-marked-compiler} therefore applies.
For components containing an additive letter, root at such a letter;
its tree estimate bounds their site sum by its inflated site price,
with the fixed support-conversion factors just paid. Enlarging to
$q_nA_s=\omega_n$ leaves room for these factors. Components with no
additive letter have site price $s^{-1/48+o(1)}$, eventually at most
$\delta_n-\omega_n$: indeed $q_n\ge s^{-1/200}$ and
$\delta_n-\omega_n=q_n(A_s^3-A_s)$. This proves (V1), with the
specified separate allowances for the two classes. It also proves
the inflated product estimate itself: use the pointwise owner and
auxiliary bounds and the single joint warning event, and then group
connected constituents. All these estimates use the full supports.

\emph{Reconstruction and renewal.}
Choose the scalar using the positive original loading, the additive
family from the regular logarithm and the correction
\eqref{eq:restart-path-price}, and the forced family by
Proposition~\ref{prop:corrected-forced-output}. Its proof, applied to
the genuine corrected body identity, supplies (V3). The absolute
component bounds just proved justify its reassembly and every
hole-deletion identity. Their normalization is the same one used by
the mixed compiler. The path bound proves (V2), so the new additive
cost is at most $s^{-1/200}+16\delta_n\le q_{n+1}$.

For completeness the hole corrections do not exhaust the forced
margin. Their raised site cost is at most
$16l\delta_n e^{16\delta_n}$ by
$|e^{-c}-1|\le e^{|c|}|c|$. The path correction has been absorbed entirely into the coarse
additive family, so there is no residual boundary factor. The component
and raised-hole site costs are consequently $s^{-1/400+o(1)}$ by
\eqref{eq:restart-decay}. They satisfy the rooted smallness and
normalization conditions of the mixed compiler, with its available
$H_s^{-1/8}$ reserve. Its forced output is bounded by $1/m$, hence
by $G(x_i)^2\le G(x_i)$. Attach and sum at full addresses as in
that proposition; this preserves the bound and all eligibility tests.
The resulting neutral law has the retained original path bounds, zero
additive cost and this new forced allowance. The symmetrization above
supplies the motion properties of the genuine additive family.
This proves the induction hypothesis at level $i$ together with the
exact push of the genuine incoming density.

Only the newly constructed entry at $i$ is installed. Thus subsequent
backward steps retain the endpoint and every already constructed
higher entry. The equality of consecutive couplings makes them all
$\beta_{N,p,k}$, and composition gives the density identities on the
same auxiliary course. For the three possible adjacent stage pairs,
use their own positive path rates, floors and clips throughout and
intersect their finitely many thresholds. The numerical argument of
Lemma~\ref{lem:restart-stage-boundary} now applies with (V1)--(V3)
proved. Finally the alphabet, loading and all-hole identities were
selected before any marked family. Decreasing the raw exponent and
inflations gives the upper and lower uses on this same output,
as asserted in Proposition~\ref{prop:allarity-compiler-reserve}.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{One auxiliary family on the whole chart window}
\label{subsec:restart-chart-family}

The family used in a chart is the one just constructed, including its
re-expressed entry at the top. We record its budget and density at every
level; equality of two endpoint representations is not needed.
Put
\[
 b(x)=\lceil(\log x)^2\rceil,\qquad
 x_j=\widehat\beta_j,\qquad b_j=b(x_j),
 \qquad B_j=b_j^2,\qquad
 \tau_j=x_j^{-1/2}\exp((\log\log x_j)^{3/2}).
\]
The radius $B_j$ is the representation's budget radius. It is distinct
from the recording radius $b(r(x_j))$ in the local Gaussian calculation.
The retiming of Subsection~\ref{sec:actual-reader-dawn-selection} is chosen
so that its recording threshold equals $\tau_j$; it satisfies
$x_j\le r(x_j)\le x_j^2$ and $b(r(x_j))\le4b_j$.
Thus, for all large $x_j$,
\[
 25b(r(x_j))+30\le b_j^2\le b(r(x_j))^2.
\]
The first inequality follows from $100b_j+30\le b_j^2$ eventually,
and the second from $b_j\le b(r(x_j))$. Also
$B_{j+1}\le2B_j$ eventually: on each fixed stage pair
$x_{j+1}=x_j+c_G\log L_j$, so $b_{j+1}/b_j\to1$.
The finite adjacent-pair purchases make these inequalities simultaneous
on the window. These are precisely the budget-radius conditions of
Subsection~\ref{sec:actual-corrected-body-compiler}. We can therefore use
the exact budgets $(B_j,\tau_j)$, rather than merely comparable radii.

\begin{proposition}[The retained auxiliary chart family]
\label{prop:restart-chart-family}
Fix a finite grade menu and an arc bound $\bar b>0$. Under the funded
hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-actual-closure}, for every
sufficiently large level $k$, every chart with arc $0<b\le\bar b$,
every $N\ge k$ and every
period $p$, choose the endpoint presentation and then the backward
steps once. Denote the resulting representations on $[w,k]$ by $P_j$.
They have budgets $(B_j,\tau_j)$, the original paths and stage clips,
and common coupling $\beta=\beta_{N,p,k}$. Their additive value costs
are bounded by $q_{k-j}$, and their densities are nonnegative.

Let $D_k$ be the density of the original selected representation at $k$,
and define, using the selected kernels at the frozen coupling $\beta$,
\[
 D_j=\mathcal K_{j,\beta}D_{j+1}\quad(w\le j<k).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restart-chart-density}
 \operatorname{eval}(P_j)=D_j\quad(w\le j\le k),\qquad
 \int D_j\,d\mu_j=Z_{N,p}.
\end{equation}
All the local compiler and marked comparisons on this chart use these
same $P_j$ and the alphabets chosen in their construction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $k$, Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-endpoint} supplies exactly the
budget $(b_k^2,\tau_k)$ while retaining the original path, clip,
coupling and density. It supplies cost at most $q_0$. In particular,
$P_k$ is a new presentation of $D_k$, not an assertion that its smaller
threshold equals the threshold of the original presentation.

Suppose the entries $P_{i+1},\ldots,P_k$ have been fixed. Apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-actual-closure} to this very incoming
$P_{i+1}$ and the original adjacent tube, with the coarse budget
$(B_i,\tau_i)$ verified above. Retain its output as $P_i$ and retain
its corrected alphabet. That proposition gives the original coarse
path and clip, coupling $\beta$, the cost $q_{k-i}$ and
\[
 \operatorname{eval}(P_i)
   =\mathcal K_{i,\beta}\operatorname{eval}(P_{i+1}).
\]
No higher entry is changed. Finite descending induction proves all
these assertions together, and the recursion defining $D_i$ proves
\eqref{eq:restart-chart-density}'s first assertion. Positivity follows
from the nonnegative endpoint and positive kernels, not from any sign
claim about the individual terms of the expansion.

Each selected kernel has mass one in its coarse variable.
The endpoint density is integrable on the finite-volume space; the
bounded positive kernels preserve integrability. Fubini therefore gives
$\int D_i\,d\mu_i=\int D_{i+1}\,d\mu_{i+1}$. At $k$ the original
selected transition identities identify this integral with $Z_{N,p}$.
This proves the second assertion. Finally the local construction chooses
its alphabet before the marked insertions and supplies both compiler
uses on that alphabet. Keeping those choices at each step gives the
last assertion. This is a construction of one family with its estimates;
it makes no uniqueness claim for representations having the same density.
\end{proof}
