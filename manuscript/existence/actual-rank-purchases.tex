\subsection{Common rank tests on the selected history}
\label{sec:actual-rank-purchases}

We now organize the comparison estimates into the finite set of tests
needed to prevent loss of rank. The essential quantifier is that one
calibration serves every row of the testing matrix. Its choice may depend
on the comparison scale and on the finite volume, but cannot depend on
the row subsequently tested. We also retain the original partition
function throughout the auxiliary integration history.

Work with the funded calibrated family for the simple gauge group of the
construction, retaining its paid calendar and local comparison inputs.
Fix a grade $d$. Let $V_d$ be its reference coefficient space, with its
real structure, distinguished unit $e_0$, and coefficient norm $|\cdot|_1$.
Fix a finite real basis $q_0,\ldots,q_r$ of the free quotient, starting
with the scalar, and real representatives $v_0,\ldots,v_r\in V_d$,
with $v_0=e_0$. Such representatives exist: lift each real quotient vector
and replace a lift $v$ by $(v+\bar v)/2$. The quotient projection commutes
with conjugation, so its image is unchanged. The scalar has its prescribed
lift. Fix also the finite generic measurement configurations separating
these basis vectors. Each row uses either one or two insertions.

The argument below uses the same-family local estimates of the preceding
construction: the corrected mixed compiler, its reader comparisons, the
calibrated insertion prices, and the constant-coupling auxiliary history.
Their numerical inequalities are imposed at the selected comparison
scales. In particular the assertion that the compiler applies to every
matched palette and that the actual reader has its required mean and
covariance bounds remains part of this input. The finite-dimensional
selection in this section does not supply a missing local estimate.

\subsubsection{Buying comparison scales before selecting the history}

Write $j$ for a stage of the construction and $k$ for a level of the
realized calendar; $J(k)$ is its stage. The preliminary purchase supplies
a finite menu $I_j$ of grades at stage $j$, with every fixed grade belonging
to $I_j$ for all sufficiently large $j$. For the finite requests in $I_j$
choose the comparison parameters and then the necessary block floor.
Two kinds of bounds enter this floor: those ensuring the local estimates
and those allowing their comparison parameters to be bought. Their
maximum, denoted $\mu_j$, serves both purposes. Increasing the preliminary
block prescription preserves these properties. Choose $L_j\ge\mu_j$.

Some parameters can only be chosen after $L_j$. For a grade $i\in I_j$,
let $a_{ij}>0$ be the admissible upper bound for its final marker radius.
Let $c_{ij}>0$ be the radius already allowed by its comparison data.
After fixing a common positive outer parameter for the finite menu, choose
\[
             0<\delta_{ij}\le\min(a_{ij},c_{ij},1).
\]
The local comparison is stable under this reduction of the marker radius.
Choose its remaining threshold after $\delta_{ij}$ and $L_j$. Taking the
maximum over $i\in I_j$ gives a single eventual coupling threshold for
the stage. No maximum over infinitely many grades is required.

The paid calendar is enlarged at these thresholds and at the existing
window and minimum-length bounds. The realization property of that
calendar says that every sufficiently late stage occurs on a paid run
of levels, with its block, coupling and window data. This proves that,
for each fixed grade, suitable comparison levels are unbounded. Indeed,
given a lower level $k_0$, choose a stage $j>J(k_0)$ in whose menu the
grade occurs, also beyond the finitely many construction floors. Realize
that stage at a level $k$. If $k\le k_0$, monotonicity of $J$ would give
$j=J(k)\le J(k_0)$, a contradiction. Thus $k>k_0$.

At these levels all the local comparisons use the same price map and
the same geometric history. To check this last point, the preliminary
shell purchase is enlarged to
\[
                     \text{shell}_j\ge100(j+7)L_j.
\]
The radius needed by the comparison is $(j+7)L_j$, and the paid volume
bound therefore supplies its factor-$100$ room. The path of the selected
history is the path of the purchased evolution at every finite cutoff.
Its funding and support-link records consequently give the comparison
on that path with precisely the original price map. This is the point
at which a local estimate is attached to the history: a second
independently realized paid calendar would not justify the substitution.

In the selected construction, these choices occur in the following
order. The finite menus and block floors are chosen first; the enlarged
shell delays and post-length thresholds come next; the common paid
calendar is realized next. Finally the callback which receives the
selected finite-volume history constructs its comparison binding using
the path identity just checked. Thus subsequent use of a frequent set
of comparison levels does not choose another trajectory.

\subsubsection{One readout and one auxiliary history for all rows}

Retain only comparison levels $k\ge\operatorname{ref}(d)$ and discard
the finitely many levels where the purchased exponent $I_k$ below is
negative. This is possible since $I_k\to\infty$. Fix such a suitable
level $k$ and write $s$ for its clock coupling. The actual
couplings obey the purchased bound
\[
                  |\beta_{N,p,k}-s|\le s/8
                  \qquad(N\ge k).
\]
Here $N$ is the mesh cutoff and $p$ the period index. At every such finite
volume the construction of Subsection~\ref{sec:restarted-rank-history}
gives a constant-coupling auxiliary history on the required window.
Its transition identities and all its local comparisons refer to the
same endpoint as the original history. Its additive remainder need only
have the value bounds stated there.

The calibrated insertion bounds hold eventually in $N$, uniformly for
every $p$, and for every row in the finite testing family. Intersect these
finitely many eventual conditions before choosing the auxiliary history.
The local readout construction then supplies a single complex-linear map
$T_{k,N,p}:V_d\to V_d$. Write $A_{k,N,p}$ for the primary-coordinate map,
including its factor $a_k^{-d}$, where $a_k>0$ is the lattice spacing.
The readout satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-common-tested}
 T_{k,N,p}e_0=e_0,\qquad
 T_{k,N,p}\bar v=\overline{T_{k,N,p}v},\qquad
 |A_{k,N,p}v-T_{k,N,p}v|_1\le\tfrac12|A_{k,N,p}v|_1.
\end{equation}
These are estimates on the reference coefficient space. They are obtained
with the same reader and mixed coefficient estimates used for the marked
integrals, not by choosing an arbitrary near-invertible map afterwards.

Here is how the previously proved comparison steps combine at this
choice. The common coordinate construction of
Subsection~\ref{sec:allarity-coordinate-calibration} gives the readout and
its covariance atlas. The actual covariance estimates of
Subsection~\ref{sec:actual-covariance-transport} apply to this atlas at the
prescribed configurations. The paid coefficient bound for every named
lift makes its terminal insertion admissible. For every row, the
healthy-field argument of Subsection~\ref{sec:marked-healthy-cumulant-launch}
then compares the actual middle-scale polynomial to the free polynomial.
Subsection~\ref{sec:allarity-cumulant-comparison} performs the connected
coefficient subtraction. The same-row marked propagation of
Subsection~\ref{sec:allarity-marked-propagation} transports this comparison
through the lower part of the auxiliary window. All finite row and
column requests are imposed before the eventual threshold is chosen.
The comparison radius and local constants can depend on $d$ and on this
finite family, but the subsequent threshold does not depend on $p$.

In more precise terms, this produces the following joint statement at
unboundedly many $k$. For all sufficiently large $N$, and every $p$, there
are one map $T_{k,N,p}$ satisfying \eqref{eq:rank-common-tested}, one
integration course, and one auxiliary representation at the lower end
of the window. For every family of lifts solving the normalization
equations below, the coefficient estimates hold for every row and column
on this same representation. The marked density identity keeps its
partition function equal to that of the original endpoint. The
quantifier over normalized lifts follows the choice of the readout;
there is no separate readout for different lift families.

Only arities at most two enter this assertion. At the middle scale the
local healthy comparison starts the required one- and two-marker
coefficients. The propagation keeps their invoice, meaning the original
marked density after subtraction of its prescribed free connected
coefficients. The normalization estimate in
Subsection~\ref{sec:rank-root-normalization} applies at the lower end of
that very integration course. If $I\ne\varnothing$ is a chosen marker
subset and $q$ is the number of terminal cells, its estimate is
\[
 a^{q|I|}|\mathbb E(\text{replicated coefficient})|
       \le \exp(q I_k),\qquad a=\exp(2I_k).
\]
The finite-volume harmonic correction is already included in $I_k$ as
specified there. Taking the $q$th root gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-common-root}
       \|\text{coefficient }I\|_{\rm root}
       \le e^{I_k-2I_k|I|}\le e^{-I_k}=:\epsilon_k.
\end{equation}
The clock purchase makes $I_k\to\infty$, so $\epsilon_k\to0$.
The empty coefficient is the unaltered scalar normalization and is not
subject to this estimate. In the two-marker coefficient the singleton
subtractions are retained; replacing it by an unsubtracted two-point
observable would not give \eqref{eq:rank-common-root}.

\subsubsection{Solving the normalization equations exactly}

The reference transport estimates give a number $D_k>0$, independent of
$N\ge k$ and $p$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-reference-inverse}
                       |v|_1\le D_k|A_{k,N,p}v|_1.
\end{equation}
Explicitly one may take
\[
 D_k=a_k^d\prod_{u=0}^{k-1} C_{u+1}L_u^d,
\]
where $C_{u+1}\ge1$ is the purchased front-column bound. This follows
by applying that bound successively from the normalized reference level
to $k$; the extra earlier factors are at least one. The factor $a_k^d$
undoes the normalization in $A_{k,N,p}$.

For brevity suppress $k,N,p$ in this paragraph. By the triangle inequality
and \eqref{eq:rank-common-tested},
\[
                         |Av|_1\le2|Tv|_1.
\]
If $Tv=0$, \eqref{eq:rank-reference-inverse} implies $v=0$.
Thus $T$ is injective and hence bijective on the finite-dimensional space
$V_d$. This also proves the inverse estimate $|v|_1\le2D_k|Tv|_1$.

Each coordinate of $V_d$ has two nonnegative integer degrees: a position
degree and a factor degree. Denote multiplication by $\rho$ to the first
degree by $W_\rho$, and multiplication by $t$ to the second by $S_t$.
They are distinct diagonal maps. Put
\[
 t_{k,N,p}=\frac{\beta_{N,p,k}^{-1/2}}{\theta_k}>0,
 \qquad \rho_k=\mathcal M_k^b,
\]
where $\theta_k$ is the original clock threshold, $\mathcal M_k$ its
monitor and $b>0$ the fixed comparison exponent. For every $i$ solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-common-lifts}
                  S_{t_{k,N,p}}T_{k,N,p}u_i=W_{\rho_k}v_i.
\end{equation}
There is exactly one solution, since both maps on the left are
invertible. Because all the maps commute with conjugation and $v_i$ is
real, conjugating the equation and using uniqueness shows $\bar u_i=u_i$.
Both diagonal maps fix $e_0$, so uniqueness and $Te_0=e_0$ give $u_0=e_0$.
All columns are therefore normalized simultaneously, with their actual
unit and real structure.

The same equations give a bound independent of the two cutoffs at fixed
$k$. The coupling band and the clock identity
$\theta_k=\widehat\beta_k^{-1/2}\mathcal M_k$ imply
\[
            t_{k,N,p}^{-1}
            =\theta_k\sqrt{\beta_{N,p,k}}\le2\mathcal M_k.
\]
Since all factor degrees are nonnegative,
\[
 |T_{k,N,p}u_i|_1
 =|S_{t_{k,N,p}^{-1}}W_{\rho_k}v_i|_1
 \le |S_{2\mathcal M_k}W_{\rho_k}v_i|_1.
\]
Together with the inverse estimate this bounds every $|u_i|_1$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-fixed-scale-bounded}
            B_k=2D_k\sum_{i=0}^r
                        |S_{2\mathcal M_k}W_{\rho_k}v_i|_1.
\end{equation}
No uniform bound in $k$ is asserted or needed. For the finitely many mesh
cutoffs preceding the common eventual threshold, define every lift to be
$e_0$. Enlarging $B_k$ by $|e_0|_1$ makes the bound valid for all $N,p$,
without changing any eventual marked estimate.

\subsubsection{Selecting a sequence and taking its ordered limits}

Let $\ell(k)$ denote the lower end of the comparison window. On the
admissible comparison levels the calendar is constant in stage throughout
$[\ell(k),k]$. Its depth tends to infinity, and its physical test scale is
\[
                       \sigma_k=a_k\mathcal M_k^b\longrightarrow0.
\]
The generic lattice choices for each fixed measurement configuration
converge, after division by $\sigma_k$, to that configuration. Their
physical coordinates lie in the middle half of a cell at level $\ell(k)$;
in particular they have the fixed relative boundary margin $1/4$.
These are the geometric fit and tuning conditions purchased for the
finite configurations. They hold eventually at admissible levels.

Intersect the unbounded set of jointly funded comparison levels with
these eventual conditions and with the eventual normalization conditions
of Subsection~\ref{sec:rank-root-normalization}. For any $k_0,\ell_0$ and
$\eta>0$ there remains a level $k$ with
\[
                  k>k_0,\qquad \ell(k)>\ell_0,
                  \qquad \sigma_k<\eta.
\]
For the middle inequality, choose a stage later than $J(\ell_0)$.
The plateau property forces its lower window end past $\ell_0$.
The other two inequalities follow from cofinality and $\sigma_k\to0$.
Choose $k_0$ in this set, and recursively choose $k_{n+1}$ with
\[
 k_{n+1}>k_n,\qquad \ell(k_{n+1})>\ell(k_n),
                      \qquad \sigma_{k_{n+1}}<\sigma_{k_n}.
\]
The physical scales are strictly decreasing and tend to zero, because
$k_n\to\infty$. The error bounds $\epsilon_{k_n}$ also tend to zero;
their monotonicity is unnecessary. Choose the simultaneous normalized
lifts just constructed at each $k_n$. Their cut-off uniform bounds are
those of \eqref{eq:rank-fixed-scale-bounded}.

At each $n$ and all sufficiently large $N$, uniformly for every period,
the insertion stencils lie in the comparison cell and every nonempty
corrected coefficient has the root bound \eqref{eq:rank-common-root}.
There are only finitely many rows and columns at fixed $d$, so their
thresholds have a common maximum. The resulting sequence thus supplies
the bounded lifts, geometry and moment estimates needed for the common
extraction of Subsection~\ref{sec:finite-rank-kernel-transport}.

Include all these bounded lift sequences, for every grade and comparison
index, in that same countable extraction. First take the mesh limit for
each fixed period, then the period limit. Denote the limiting lifts by
$u_i^{(n)}$. Reality and the unit identity survive these limits. The
finite-cell moment inequality and the corrected root bound give, for
every row and column,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-purchased-point-comparison}
 \left|S(\sigma_{k_n}x^{(n)};,u^{(n)})
                 -S_{\rm free}(x^{(n)};,q)\right|
             \le P_n\epsilon_{k_n}.
\end{equation}
Here $S$ is the extracted interacting correlation, $q$ lists the free
basis elements used by the row, and $x^{(n)}$ is its rescaled lattice
configuration. The factor $P_n$ is the finite polynomial in the absolute
free subset moments appearing when the connected subtractions are
undone. It is bounded: $x^{(n)}$ tends to the fixed generic configuration,
all its subsets remain separated there, and the free correlations are
continuous on those domains. Hence the right side tends to zero.

To justify the order of this passage, fix $n$ first. Its root bound holds
for every period and all mesh cutoffs past one threshold. Apply the
finite-cell inequality at those cutoffs; joint convergence of the lifts
and correlations gives the inequality after the mesh limit at each
period, and then after the period limit. Only then let $n\to\infty$.
There is no interchange between the comparison-scale limit and either
finite-volume limit. Equation~\eqref{eq:rank-purchased-point-comparison}
is exactly the finite measurement convergence used in the rank argument.
Identification of the entire equation kernel additionally requires the
all-spectator local equation comparison stated in that argument; finite
one- and two-point measurements alone do not supply it.

\subsubsection{The same construction on a trace chart window}
\label{subsec:rank-trace-chart-window}

The pairwise receipt of the trace calibration uses two fields of its
chart witness, (F3) and (F5) of Subsection~\ref{sec:ex-lattice-firesky-gallery}.
We construct the constant-coupling auxiliary history on the chart window
$[w,k]$ and the tested readout at level $k$ for every sufficiently large
$k$. The remaining witness requirements are stated below.

Fix a grade $d$, a bound $\bar b$ for the base arcs, and the finite menu
of the trace tests at grade $d$. It has arities at most two, and the
marker allowance $M=d+2$ enters only through the marker weight, not
through the history or the readout. For a chart at level $k$ with base
arc $b\le\bar b$, let $\ell$ and $w$ be its middle level and root depth.
Write $s=\widehat\beta_k$ and $y=\log\log s$.

We use the running estimate of the selected couplings relative to the
clock line. Let $\bar\beta$ be the intercept of
Subsection~\ref{sec:surviving-interval-shooting} and $b$ the prescribed
level-zero coupling of the selected histories, so that
$|b-\bar\beta|\le\bar\beta/2$, and put $c_R=|b-\bar\beta|+|\bar\beta-\beta_*|$.

\begin{lemma}[The running estimate on the clock line]
\label{lem:rank-trace-running}
For every $\eta>0$ there is $m_0(\eta)$ such that
\[
 |\beta_{N,p,m}-\widehat\beta_m|\le c_R+\eta s_m
\]
for all $m\ge m_0(\eta)$, all $N\ge m$ and all $p$. Here
$\widehat\beta_m=\beta_*+s_Gs_m$ is the clock of
Subsection~\ref{sec:common-scale-selection}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In the notation of Subsection~\ref{sec:surviving-interval-shooting}, the
block side of level $i$ is $L_i=L_{J(i+1)}$ and the stage allowance is
$\varepsilon_{i+1}=\varepsilon_{J(i+1)}$, as recorded at the end of
the drift paragraph of Subsection~\ref{sec:common-scale-selection}. Since
$s_0=0$ and $s_{i+1}=s_i+\log L_{J(i+1)}$, the accumulated scale of that
section is $S_i=s_i$, and its line is
$\bar\beta+s_GS_i=\widehat\beta_i+(\bar\beta-\beta_*)$. The selected
history at cutoff $N$ and period $p$ is a shooting history of that
section, with level-zero coupling $b$
(Subsection~\ref{sec:common-funded-history}). Hence
\eqref{eq:shoot-running-error} gives
\[
 |\beta_{N,p,i}-\widehat\beta_i|
   \le E_i+|b-\bar\beta|+|\bar\beta-\beta_*|=E_i+c_R ,
\]
with $E_i=\sum_{j<i}\varepsilon_{j+1}$, which depends only on the
schedule, not on $N$ or $p$. The drift condition
\eqref{eq:shoot-drift-ratio} holds on the selected schedule. As shown at
the end of that section, for given $\eta>0$ choose $j$ with
$J_{i+1}^{-2}\le\eta/2$ for $i\ge j$. Then
$E_i\le E_j+\tfrac\eta2(s_i-s_j)$, and since $s_i\to\infty$, eventually
$E_j\le\tfrac\eta2s_i$. So $E_i\le\eta s_i$ for all $i$ beyond a
threshold depending only on $\eta$.
\end{proof}

The slope here is the clock slope $s_G$, which is the slope of the
shooting construction by definition. Its identification with the one-loop
coefficient $2b_0(G)$ is needed for the public running consequence, not
here.

\begin{lemma}[The endpoint coupling bound]
\label{lem:rank-trace-endpoint}
For all sufficiently large $k$, every $N\ge k$ and every $p$,
\[
 |\beta_{N,p,k}-\widehat\beta_k|\le\widehat\beta_k/8 .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take $\eta=s_G/16$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-trace-running}. Since
$s_k\to\infty$, eventually $c_R\le s_Gs_k/16$. Then the right side there
is at most $s_Gs_k/8\le\widehat\beta_k/8$, because
$\widehat\beta_k=\beta_*+s_Gs_k\ge s_Gs_k$.
\end{proof}

Two further receipts are needed on the chart window. The restarted
construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-actual-closure} supplies
the first, and Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-selected-tested-readout} below
supplies the second. We state their precise content, including the four
properties used in Sections~\ref{sec:allarity-coordinate-calibration},
\ref{sec:allarity-cumulant-comparison}
and~\ref{sec:marked-healthy-cumulant-launch}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(V)] \emph{Restarted steps of the window.} At every step of the
 window $[w,k]$, from a level $m+1$ to $m$, the restarted step at the
 constant coupling $\beta_{N,p,k}$, with the clips, budget radius and
 support data of its stage pair $(J(m+1),J(m))$, satisfies the component
 bounds (V1), the path correction (V2) and the reassembly (V3) of
 Subsection~\ref{subsec:restart-stage-boundary}. The pair is one of
 $(J(k),J(k))$, $(J(k),J(k)-1)$ and $(J(k)-1,J(k)-1)$. The purchases of
 Subsection~\ref{sec:common-funded-history} concern the selected history and
 are supplemented by the explicit restarted-step estimates of
 Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-actual-closure}; the selected-step bounds
 alone would not supply them.
\item[(T)] \emph{Calibrated insertion receipt.} At every sufficiently
 large level $k$, on the same endpoint, for all sufficiently large $N$
 uniformly in $p$, and for every grade $d'\le d$, there are an actual
 polynomial map $\mathcal H=\mathcal H_{k,N,p}$, assigning to each label
 $u$ of grade $d'$ a local polynomial insertion $\mathcal Hu$ in the reader
 coordinates, and a linear map $T=T_{k,N,p}$ with the following
 properties.
 \begin{enumerate}
 \item[(T1)] \emph{Low-grade reading.} $T$ is the low-grade reading of
  $\mathcal H$. For every label $u$, the grade truncation of $\mathcal Hu$,
  after substitution of the actual linear reader map at level $k$, is the
  free polynomial whose frame coefficients are $Tu$ in the retained common
  normalization of Subsection~\ref{sec:allarity-coordinate-calibration},
  that is, rescaled by the coupling factor of the reader coordinates at
  level $k$. In particular, for the lifts
  of \eqref{eq:rank-common-lifts} the exact low-grade identity of
  Subsection~\ref{sec:allarity-cumulant-comparison} holds.
 \item[(T2)] \emph{Tested.} $T$ satisfies \eqref{eq:rank-common-tested}:
  $Te_0=e_0$, $T\bar v=\overline{Tv}$, and
  $|A_{k,N,p}v-Tv|_1\le\frac12|A_{k,N,p}v|_1$.
 \item[(T3)] \emph{Primal bound.} $|\mathcal Hu|_1\le H|A_{k,N,p}u|_1$,
  with $H=\mathcal M_k$, and the degree of $\mathcal Hu$ is bounded
  independently of $k,N,p$.
 \item[(T4)] \emph{Stamp.} There is a raw curvature polynomial $W_u$ with
  $\mathcal H u=\mathcal W_{K_k}W_u$, where $K_k$ is the original
  same-slot atlas covariance. Its physical evaluation has the healthy
  approximation and paid coefficient bounds, on the halo of its reader,
  required for the terminal birth term in
  Subsection~\ref{sec:marked-healthy-cumulant-launch}. The Gaussian
  calculation uses $\mathcal H u$ and subtracts $K_k$ from each
  diagonal covariance block, as in
  Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-raw-wick-identity}.
 \end{enumerate}
 The same map $\mathcal H$ and the same map $T$ occur in all four
 clauses. The polynomials $\mathcal Hu$ are used in the Gaussian
 cumulant comparison and $T$ is their reading. The physical terminal
 stamp is paid by the raw polynomials $W_u$ from which these same
 $\mathcal Hu$ are obtained; the covariance conversion is retained.
\end{enumerate}
The map $T=A_{k,N,p}$ satisfies (T2) trivially when $A_{k,N,p}$ fixes the
unit and is real, but it need not satisfy (T1). The readout must be the
exact low-grade reading of the insertions that are actually integrated,
because the free cumulant comparison uses the exact low-grade identity.
The content of (T2) for the reading of (T1) is therefore an estimate. It
says that the primary coordinate of the selected history at level $k$
agrees, to relative accuracy one half, with the low-grade reading of the
polynomial insertion built from the same label. Given (T1) and that
estimate, the unit and reality clauses follow from $\mathcal He_0=1$ and
from the reality of $\mathcal H$.

The following lemma reduces that estimate to a \emph{pre-pin} estimate for
the plain insertion before normal ordering, read through the actual
nonlinear reader substitution. The two changes between that reading and
the reading of (T1) are perturbative. On polynomials we use the
coefficient $\ell^1$ norm $|\cdot|_1$. It is submultiplicative, and
$|1|_1=1$.

\begin{lemma}[Reduction of the pin]
\label{lem:rank-trace-pin-reduction}
Fix $k,N,p$ and a grade $d'\le d$, and constants $D\in\mathbb N$,
$B\ge1$, $c\ge1$, $0<t\le1$ and $0\le\varepsilon\le1$. Suppose that for
every label $u$ of grade $d'$ the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal Hu=\mathcal N_Kp_u$, where $p_u$ is a polynomial of degree
 at most $D$ in the reader variables $y_i$ with $|p_u|_1\le B|A_{k,N,p}u|_1$.
 Here $\mathcal N_K=\exp(-\frac12\Delta_K)$, with
 $\Delta_K=\sum_{i,j}K_{ij}\partial_{y_i}\partial_{y_j}$, is normal
 ordering with respect to a symmetric matrix $K$ whose entries satisfy
 $|K_{ij}|\le\varepsilon$.
\item In the rescaled frame coordinates, the actual reader substitution
 sends $y_i$ to $\ell_i+ta_i$. Here $\ell_i$ is linear, every monomial of
 $a_i$ has degree at least $2$, and $|\ell_i|_1\le B$, $|a_i|_1\le B$.
\item The reading $\Lambda$, grade truncation followed by passage to frame
 coefficients, satisfies $|\Lambda q|_1\le c|q|_1$.
\item \emph{Pre-pin.}
 $|A_{k,N,p}u-\Lambda\bigl(p_u\circ(\ell+ta)\bigr)|_1\le\varepsilon|A_{k,N,p}u|_1$.
\end{enumerate}
Let $Tu=\Lambda\bigl((\mathcal Hu)\circ\ell\bigr)$ be the reading of
(T1). Then
\[
 |A_{k,N,p}u-Tu|_1\le(\varepsilon+cB\,a_D)\,|A_{k,N,p}u|_1,\qquad
 a_D=DtB(2B)^D+\varepsilon\,w_DB^D,\quad w_D=\tfrac{D^2}2e^{D^2/2}.
\]
In particular, if $\varepsilon+cBa_D\le\frac12$, then the estimate in (T2)
holds at grade $d'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $A=A_{k,N,p}$ and $p=p_u$. Three bounds are used, each for a
polynomial $q$ of degree at most $D$.

\emph{Linear substitution.} A monomial of degree $m\le D$ is sent to a
product of $m$ linear forms, of norm at most $B^m\le B^D$. Hence
$|q\circ\ell|_1\le B^D|q|_1$.

\emph{Nonlinear correction.} For a monomial $y_{i_1}\cdots y_{i_m}$,
telescoping gives
\[
 \prod_{r=1}^m(\ell_{i_r}+ta_{i_r})-\prod_{r=1}^m\ell_{i_r}
 =\sum_{r=1}^m\prod_{r'<r}(\ell_{i_{r'}}+ta_{i_{r'}})\;ta_{i_r}
  \prod_{r'>r}\ell_{i_{r'}} .
\]
Each summand has norm at most $(2B)^{m-1}tB$, since $t\le1$. Hence
$|q\circ(\ell+ta)-q\circ\ell|_1\le DtB(2B)^D|q|_1$.

\emph{Normal ordering.} For a monomial $y^\alpha$ of degree $m$,
$\partial_i\partial_jy^\alpha=\alpha_i(\alpha_j-\delta_{ij})y^{\alpha-e_i-e_j}$
vanishes unless $i,j$ lie in the support of $\alpha$. So
$|\Delta_Ky^\alpha|_1\le\varepsilon\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\alpha_j=\varepsilon
m^2$, independently of the number of variables. Since $\Delta_K$ lowers the
degree, $|\frac12\Delta_Kq|_1\le\frac12\varepsilon D^2|q|_1$ on polynomials
of degree at most $D$. Summing the exponential series,
\[
 |\mathcal N_Kq-q|_1\le\bigl(e^{\varepsilon D^2/2}-1\bigr)|q|_1
 \le\varepsilon w_D|q|_1 ,
\]
by $e^x-1\le xe^x$ with $x=\varepsilon D^2/2\le D^2/2$. Also $\mathcal N_Kq$
has degree at most $D$.

Now
\[
 Au-Tu=\bigl[Au-\Lambda(p\circ(\ell+ta))\bigr]
  +\Lambda\bigl(p\circ(\ell+ta)-p\circ\ell\bigr)
  +\Lambda\bigl((p-\mathcal N_Kp)\circ\ell\bigr).
\]
By the pre-pin estimate, the first term has norm at most
$\varepsilon|Au|_1$. By item~3 and the two bounds above, the others have
norm at most $c\,DtB(2B)^D|p|_1$ and $cB^D\varepsilon w_D|p|_1$. Since
$|p|_1\le B|Au|_1$, the claim follows.
\end{proof}

\subparagraph{The polynomial pre-pin and its normalization.}
The primary coefficient space and the polynomial jet space have different
norms. Write $E:V_d\to\mathscr P_d$ for exposure in the fixed radial jet
frame, where $\mathscr P_d$ is the space of polynomials of weighted grade
at most $d$. The weights of the finitely many jet variables are positive
integers. Fix $c_d\ge1$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-exposure-cost}
 |v|_1\le c_d|Ev|_1\qquad(v\in V_d).
\end{equation}
Such a constant exists because $E$ is injective between fixed
finite-dimensional spaces. It does not depend on the lattice volume.
A bound for $|EAu-q|_1$ is therefore not yet a bound with the same
constant for the corresponding frame coefficients.

Here is the precise near/wide reduction. Its hypotheses distinguish the
regular-germ identity from the two analytic ray estimates that are
needed to use that identity.
\begin{lemma}[Near and wide Taylor readings]\label{lem:rank-near-wide-prepin}
Fix $D\ge d$. There are finitely many real jet points
$x_1,\ldots,x_J$ and $a_d\ge1$ such that
$|q|_1\le a_d\max_j|q(x_j)|$ for $q\in\mathscr P_d$.
Let $L_u=P_u+Q_u$ be polynomials of degree at most $D$ in local link
coordinates, and let $\mathscr U$ be the linear operation taking their
ordinary radial reading and truncating to weighted grade $d$.
Suppose $M_u,\beta,C_b,\tau,\eta\ge0$ and, for each $j$, a linear
functional $\mathscr R_j$ on those polynomials satisfy
\begin{align}
 |P_u|_1&\le3\beta M_u,& |Q_u|_1&\le\eta\beta M_u,
       \label{eq:rank-near-wide-cost}\\
 \mathscr R_j(P_u)&=(EAu)(x_j),
       \label{eq:rank-near-germ}\\
 |\mathscr R_j(P_u)-(\mathscr U P_u)(x_j)|
       &\le C_b\tau|P_u|_1,&
 |(\mathscr U Q_u)(x_j)|&\le C_b|Q_u|_1.
       \label{eq:rank-ray-comparison}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-polynomial-prepin}
 |EAu-\mathscr U L_u|_1
 \le3a_d C_b\beta M_u(\tau+\eta),\qquad
 |L_u|_1\le(3+\eta)\beta M_u.
\end{equation}
The estimate is uniform in every parameter for which the displayed
hypotheses hold with the same constants.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The real evaluations separate $\mathscr P_d$: a polynomial vanishing
on all real points is zero, as follows by successively treating it as
a polynomial in each variable. In a finite-dimensional dual space a
spanning set contains a finite basis. Choose evaluations forming such
a basis, and let $q_j$ be the corresponding interpolation polynomials.
Then $q=\sum_jq(x_j)q_j$, so one may take
$a_d=\max(1,\sum_j|q_j|_1)$. If there are no jet variables, evaluation
of the constant polynomial suffices.

Both $EAu$ and $\mathscr U L_u$ belong to $\mathscr P_d$. At $x_j$,
linearity and \eqref{eq:rank-near-germ} give
\[
 (EAu-\mathscr U L_u)(x_j)
 =\mathscr R_j(P_u)-(\mathscr U P_u)(x_j)
                         -(\mathscr U Q_u)(x_j).
\]
Its modulus is at most
$C_b\beta M_u(3\tau+\eta)\le3C_b\beta M_u(\tau+\eta)$.
Apply the sampling inequality. The last estimate is the triangle
inequality and \eqref{eq:rank-near-wide-cost}.
\end{proof}

In the intended application $P_u$ sums the short-support terms of the
actual regular insertion, and $Q_u$ sums the remaining terms.
The functional $\mathscr R_j$ is its degree-$d$ reading along the
threshold-dependent holonomy ray. Equation~\eqref{eq:rank-near-germ}
uses the prescribed local-section jet and the vanishing regular
remainder jet, with all short supports inside the nonwrapping patch.
It is an exact identity about this insertion; finite-difference
reconstruction of a closed cochain alone does not imply it.
The ordinary radial reading $\mathscr U$ uses the linear edge integrals
of the radial connection. The first estimate in
\eqref{eq:rank-ray-comparison} compares the two ray readings; the wide
part only needs the second, absolute bound. In particular, after this
comparison the reader in Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-trace-pin-reduction} may
have $a_i=0$. Its nonlinear correction need not be paid a second time.

\begin{corollary}[Paying the pre-pin with growing coordinate costs]
\label{cor:rank-prepin-payment}
Let $H=\mathcal M_k\to\infty$, fix $D$, and put
$\alpha=1/(4(D+2))$ and $\gamma=\alpha/4$. Suppose the data of
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-near-wide-prepin} satisfy, uniformly in $N,p,u$,
\[
 \beta\le H^\gamma,\qquad M_u\le H^\gamma|Au|_1,
 \qquad 3a_dC_b\le H^\gamma,\qquad
 \tau\le H^{-4},\quad\eta\le H^{-4}.
\]
Suppose also that converting $L_u$ to the plain reader polynomial
$p_u$ uses a linear substitution whose rows have coefficient norm at
most $J_k\ge1$, with $J_k^D\le H^\gamma$, and preserves its truncated
reading: $\mathscr U L_u=\Pi_d(p_u\circ\ell)$.
Then, eventually and uniformly in $N,p,u$,
\[
 |EAu-\Pi_d(p_u\circ\ell)|_1\le H^{-1}|Au|_1,
 \qquad \deg p_u\le D,\qquad |p_u|_1\le H^\alpha|Au|_1.
\]
Here $\Pi_d$ denotes weighted grade truncation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first bound in \eqref{eq:rank-polynomial-prepin} is at most
$2H^{3\gamma-4}|Au|_1$. Since $3\gamma<3$, this is at most
$H^{-1}|Au|_1$ eventually. Since $\eta\le1$ eventually, its second
bound gives $|L_u|_1\le4H^{2\gamma}|Au|_1$. Substitution multiplies
this by at most $J_k^D$, by the monomial argument in
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-trace-pin-reduction}, and does not increase degree.
Finally $4H^{3\gamma}\le H^\alpha$ eventually because
$\alpha-3\gamma=\gamma>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Wick reading with scale-dependent bounds]
\label{lem:rank-growing-wick-pin}
Let $E,c_d,D,\alpha,H$ be as above and set $B=H^\alpha$, $\epsilon=H^{-1}$.
Suppose $p_u$ is linear in $u$, has degree at most $D$, and obeys
$|p_u|_1\le B|Au|_1$. Let $K$ be a symmetric matrix with
$|K_{ij}|\le\epsilon$, and let
$\mathcal W_K=\exp(\tfrac12\Delta_K)$ be the signed Gaussian
polynomial operator. Suppose $|\ell_i|_1,|a_i|_1\le B$,
$0<t\le H^{-1}$, and
\[
 |EAu-\Pi_d(p_u\circ(\ell+ta))|_1\le\epsilon|Au|_1.
\]
Assume the exact frame-membership condition
$\Pi_d((\mathcal W_Kp_u)\circ\ell)\in E(V_d)$ for all $u$.
Define $T$ uniquely by
\[
 ETu=\Pi_d((\mathcal W_Kp_u)\circ\ell).
\]
Then $T$ is linear and, eventually,
\[
 |Au-Tu|_1\le\tfrac12|Au|_1,\qquad
 |\mathcal W_Kp_u|_1\le H|Au|_1,
 \qquad\deg\mathcal W_Kp_u\le D.
\]
If $p_{e_0}=1$, $Ee_0=1$, and the construction commutes with complex
conjugation, then $Te_0=e_0$ and $T\bar u=\overline{Tu}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All operations defining $ET$ are linear and $E$ is injective, which
proves existence, uniqueness and linearity of $T$ under the stated
membership condition. Truncation has coefficient norm at most one.
The three-term decomposition in Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-trace-pin-reduction},
now made in polynomial coefficients before applying
\eqref{eq:rank-exposure-cost}, gives
\[
 |Au-Tu|_1\le c_d\{\epsilon+B[DtB(2B)^D+
                       \epsilon w_DB^D]\}|Au|_1,
 \qquad w_D=\tfrac{D^2}{2}e^{D^2/2}.
\]
The Wick estimate is unchanged for the positive sign in
$\mathcal W_K$: it used absolute values of the exponential series.
With the present choices the expression in braces is at most
\[
 H^{-1}+D2^D H^{-1+\alpha(D+2)}
                      +w_D H^{-1+\alpha(D+1)}.
\]
Both latter exponents are at most $-3/4$, since
$\alpha(D+2)=1/4$. Thus the expression tends to zero despite the growth
of $B$, and its product with the fixed $c_d$ is eventually at most
$1/2$. The same series gives
$|\mathcal W_Kp_u|_1\le(1+\epsilon w_D)B|Au|_1$ and preserves degree.
This is at most $H|Au|_1$ eventually since $\alpha<1$.
Finally $\mathcal W_K1=1$, linear substitution and truncation fix $1$,
and injectivity of $E$ proves the unit and conjugation statements.
\end{proof}

The covariance sign and coupling normalization in this last lemma are
part of its application. If $q_u$ is the unscaled insertion, put
$g_{k,N,p}=\beta_{N,p,k}^{-1/2}$ and
$z=\theta_k/g_{k,N,p}=t_{k,N,p}^{-1}$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-trace-endpoint} and the clock identity,
$z=\mathcal M_k\sqrt{\beta_{N,p,k}/\widehat\beta_k}
\ge\sqrt{7/8}\,\mathcal M_k\ge1$ for all sufficiently large $k$,
uniformly in $N\ge k$ and $p$. Set $p_u(y)=q_u(zy)$. For the actual signed
covariance $\Gamma$, differentiation gives the exact identity
\[
 (\mathcal W_\Gamma q_u)(zy)
       =\mathcal W_{z^{-2}\Gamma}p_u(y).
\]
Indeed each pair of derivatives contributes $z^{-2}$ after this
change of variables; sum the finite exponential series. Thus the $K$
in the lemma is $z^{-2}\Gamma$. Normal ordering with covariance $C$
corresponds to $\Gamma=-C$, not to changing the sign silently.
Undoing the dilation $z\ge1$ cannot increase coefficient norm, so the
primal bound also holds for the unscaled Wick insertion. Dilation
commutes with the linear reader and weighted truncation; the resulting
frame dilation is precisely the coupling rescaling in (T1).

The following construction supplies the Taylor reader, its frame membership
and its raw terminal approximation from explicit calibrated-list data.
It also distinguishes the covariance used in the coefficient reading
from the polynomial evaluated on a physical field.

\paragraph*{Constructing the raw polynomial and its Gaussian reading}

The terminal approximation and its Gaussian reading use two related
polynomials. We distinguish them before giving the estimates. For a
symmetric matrix $K$ write $\mathcal W_K=\exp(\Delta_K/2)$, where
$\Delta_K=\sum_{a,b}K_{ab}\partial_a\partial_b$. This is a finite
sum on every polynomial; neither positivity nor a probability measure
is needed. Write $W_u$ for the raw curvature polynomial and
$\mathcal H u=\mathcal W_K W_u$ for its Gaussian reading. The healthy
field is evaluated in $W_u$, not in $\mathcal H u$.

\begin{lemma}[Changing the diagonal covariance]
\label{lem:rank-raw-wick-identity}
Let $W_i$ be polynomials on separate, labelled copies of finite coordinate
spaces. Let $C$ be any symmetric matrix on their direct sum and
$K=\operatorname{diag}(K_i)$ a block diagonal symmetric matrix. For every
mean $\mu$,
\[
 \mathcal W_{C,\mu}\Bigl(\prod_iW_i\Bigr)
 =\mathcal W_{C-K,\mu}\Bigl(\prod_i\mathcal W_{K_i}W_i\Bigr).
\]
The identity holds simultaneously for every subset of the slots, with
all matrices restricted from the same direct sum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All constant-coefficient partial derivatives commute. On the space of
polynomials of degree at most the total degree of the product they are
nilpotent, so the finite binomial formula gives
$\exp(\Delta_{C-K}/2)\exp(\Delta_K/2)=\exp(\Delta_C/2)$.
Each block of $\Delta_K$ differentiates only its own factor. Hence its
exponential sends the product to $\prod_i\mathcal W_{K_i}W_i$.
Evaluate the resulting polynomial at $\mu$. The same proof on any
subset proves the last assertion, including the empty subset.
\end{proof}

For the atlas of Subsection~\ref{sec:actual-covariance-transport}, take
$K_i$ to be the original same-slot covariance $\kappa_k$.
The conditional covariance before this change is
$C_{ij}=\kappa_k(z_i+\cdot,z_j+\cdot)-B_{ij}$, with
$B=\mathcal L\kappa_\ell\mathcal L^{\mathsf T}$.
Then $C-K$ is exactly \eqref{eq:actual-normalordered-covariance}:
its diagonal is $-B_{ii}$ and its off-diagonal is $\kappa_k-B_{ij}$.
Thus the Gaussian cumulant calculation uses $\mathcal H u$ with that
subtracted covariance, whereas its terminal physical approximation uses
$W_u$. Repeated physical coordinates cause no difficulty: keep a separate
slot copy and use their full cross-covariance. This identity is an identity
of polynomial transports, not an assertion that a cutoff nonlinear
sector integral is already Gaussian.

\subparagraph{The Taylor polynomial of the calibrated regular list.}
Here are explicit sufficient data for this construction, retaining the
actual regular list and its original supports. At one level let
$\theta$ be its original health threshold, let $b$ be a patch radius,
and suppose the patch is nonwrapping. In logarithmic link coordinates
put $U(x)_e=\exp(\theta x_e)$ inside the patch and $U(x)_e=1$ outside.
Use the maximum of the colour norms for $x$. For a label $u$ split its
regular list into a near list and a wide list, with weighted terms
$c_a f_a$. Require the regularity of Subsection~\ref{sec:local-splitting}
through order $D+1$, where $D\ge d$. If $e_a$ is the corresponding
nonnegative direction price, the calibrated bounds needed are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-list-input}
 \sum_{a\text{ near}}|c_a|e_a\le3M_u,\qquad
 \sum_{a\text{ wide}}|c_a|e_a\le\eta M_u,\qquad
 0\le\eta\le1.
\end{equation}
All near supports lie in the patch. The local-section terms have
prescribed degree-$d$ projection and the other near terms have zero
projection. Their weighted projections sum to $Au$. On configurations
with no flags, the sum of both lists is the actual calibrated field,
linear in $u$, real under conjugation, invariant under constant gauge
rotations, and equal to one at $u=e_0$.

These conditions concern unmasked regular functions and their original
supports. They do not differentiate a health indicator. In the calibrated
coefficient representation the near list consists of the unit, the
fixed local-section terms, and regular terms with covering number at
most $m$. The wide list consists of the remaining regular terms.
If their exponential mass is at most $M_u$ with exponent $e>0$,
then its wide unweighted mass is at most $e^{-em}M_u$: multiply each
wide price by $e^{-em}e^{e q_a}\ge1$, since $q_a>m$.
The unit and section prices cost at most $2M_u$, and the remaining
near prices at most $M_u$. This proves \eqref{eq:rank-list-input}
from those calibrated mass bounds. The zero projections are precisely
the regular-remainder condition of Subsection~\ref{sec:local-splitting}.

\begin{lemma}[Taylor reader from a summable regular list]
\label{lem:rank-list-taylor}
Under these data there are polynomials $P_u,Q_u,L_u=P_u+Q_u$ of
degree at most $D$ in the patch link coordinates, such that
\[
 |P_u|_1\le3C_D(1+n_b)^D M_u,\qquad
 |Q_u|_1\le C_D(1+n_b)^D\eta M_u,
\]
where $n_b$ is the number of real link coordinates and $C_D$ depends
only on $D$ and the colour norm. On a fixed ball $\|x\|\le\rho_G$
with no flags,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-list-taylor-error}
 |F_u(U(x))-L_u(x)|\le4M_u\|x\|^{D+1}.
\end{equation}
The map $u\mapsto L_u$ is linear, real, unit preserving and invariant
under simultaneous adjoint rotation of its link variables. Moreover,
the near polynomial satisfies \eqref{eq:rank-near-germ}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\rho_G\le1/8$ small enough that
$\operatorname{chord}(\exp X)\le2\|X\|$ on the required colour
ball. Then every link of $U(x)$ has chord at most $\theta/4$ and
every plaquette has chord at most $\theta$; the field has no flags.
For $\|v\|\le1$, the curve $r\mapsto f_a(U(rv))$ on
$0\le r\le\rho_G$ has derivative of order $j\le D+1$ equal to
its repeated left derivative in the single direction $\theta v$.
Indeed on each link
$\exp(\theta(r+h)v_e)=\exp(\theta h v_e)\exp(\theta r v_e)$;
induct on $j$. Its derivative is bounded by $e_a$. Taylor's integral
remainder therefore bounds the error by
$e_a\|x\|^{D+1}/(D+1)!\le e_a\|x\|^{D+1}$.

Let $p_a$ be this degree-$D$ Taylor polynomial. To bound its
coefficients, polarize each homogeneous term. For a symmetric
$j$-linear form $B$, its values on coordinate vectors are bounded
by $j^j/j!$ times its diagonal supremum on the unit ball: apply
\[
 B(v_1,\ldots,v_j)=\frac1{2^j j!}
   \sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^j}
        \Bigl(\prod_i\varepsilon_i\Bigr)
        B\Bigl(\sum_i\varepsilon_i v_i,\ldots,
               \sum_i\varepsilon_i v_i\Bigr).
\]
This formula follows by expansion; every term except the one using
each $v_i$ once cancels. Coordinate vectors have norm one. Expand
the Taylor polynomial using ordered coordinate tuples; there are
$n_b^j$ such tuples at order $j$. Summing for $0\le j\le D$ gives
$|p_a|_1\le C_D(1+n_b)^D e_a$.
Consequently both series $\sum c_a p_a$ converge absolutely in the
finite-dimensional degree-$D$ polynomial space. Define $P_u,Q_u$
by these two sums. Summing the remainder bounds proves
\eqref{eq:rank-list-taylor-error} and the coefficient estimates.
This also justifies applying any fixed jet functional term by term.

A polynomial of degree at most $D$ which is
$O(\|x\|^{D+1})$ at zero is zero: restrict to every real line
and compare coefficients in its scalar parameter. Thus the Taylor
polynomial is unique. Apply uniqueness to the actual field's
linearity, real structure, constant-gauge invariance and unit to
obtain the corresponding properties of $L_u$. No linear choice of
the intermediate regular lists is required.

Finally restrict to a fixed threshold holonomy ray through zero.
Its link logarithm vanishes at zero. Composing the remainder with
this smooth curve gives $O(|r|^{D+1})$, which has zero derivatives
through order $d\le D$. Each near term's degree-$d$ reading is
therefore the reading of its Taylor polynomial. The nonwrapping
local section has its prescribed reading and each regular remainder
has zero reading, by Subsection~\ref{sec:local-splitting}. Absolute
coefficient summability permits the fixed finite jet functional to
pass through the sum. Their weighted sum is $EAu$, proving
\eqref{eq:rank-near-germ}.
\end{proof}

The remaining ray estimate also follows at the level of these actual
polynomials. For each of the finitely many fixed radial tests let
$Z_\theta(r)$ be its link logarithm divided by $\theta$, and let
$Z_0(r)$ be its linearized link integral. The logarithmic endpoint
map constructed in Subsection~\ref{sec:local-splitting} is analytic,
vanishes at zero and has that link integral as its derivative.
Its terms of degree at least two have, after division by $\theta$,
at least one remaining factor of $\theta$. Through any fixed
$r$-derivative order the connection is a fixed polynomial in the
link location. Thus, on links in the radius-$b$ patch, the jets of
$Z_\theta,Z_0$ through order $d$ are at most $C(1+b)^v$, and those
of their difference are at most $C(1+b)^v\theta$. More explicitly,
only Taylor terms of the endpoint map of order at most $d$ can
contribute: the test connection vanishes at $r=0$; its coefficients
are polynomials of bounded degree in the location. Each contributing
coefficient is a finite sum of products of those polynomials and
fixed derivatives of the endpoint map. Taking their degrees and
absolute coefficient sums gives the stated $C,v$.
For a monomial of degree at most $D$, Leibniz' rule for its
$r$-derivatives is a finite sum over distributions of at most $d$
derivatives among at most $D$ factors. Subtract products by replacing
one factor at a time. Every difference contains one jet of
$Z_\theta-Z_0$, hence one factor $\theta$; all other factors have
the preceding polynomial bound. Summing the monomial coefficients
proves \eqref{eq:rank-ray-comparison} with $C_b=C(1+b)^v$ and
$\tau=\theta$, independently of the number of links.

\subparagraph{Invariant-frame membership.}
The original atlas covariance has the form
$K_{(x,a),(y,b)}=k_{xy}\delta_{ab}$ with real $k_{xy}$, by
\eqref{eq:actual-chart-sum} and the reality proof in
Subsection~\ref{sec:actual-covariance-fourier-window}.
Its restriction to any set retaining every colour has the same
form. Since the adjoint colour action is orthogonal,
$(I\otimes\operatorname{Ad}_g)K(I\otimes\operatorname{Ad}_g)^T=K$.
The chain rule consequently shows that $\Delta_K$, and hence
$\mathcal W_K$, commutes with that action; its real coefficients
also commute with conjugation. The axial conversion of link
variables into sums of based plaquette variables is real and
colour equivariant. The Taylor lemma therefore makes $W_u$ and
$\mathcal H u$ invariant and real in the corresponding senses.
The radial reading is colour equivariant as well, and the colour
action preserves each weighted grade. Its truncation is thus an
invariant polynomial of weighted grade at most $d$, precisely an
element of the label space defined in Subsection~\ref{sec:local-splitting}.
This proves the frame-membership hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-growing-wick-pin} for this atlas; small entries
alone would not prove it.

\subparagraph{Healthy-field approximation by the raw polynomial.}
Use the axial paths based at the origin in the nonwrapping patch.
Each link is a product of at most $4b$ based plaquette holonomies.
Let $\tau$ be the health threshold at which the terminal insertion
is used, and put $a=4b\tau$. There are fixed $c_G,C_G>0$ such that,
when $a\le c_G$, the normalized logarithmic link vector $x$ and
the linear sum $y$ of the same plaquette curvatures divided by
$\theta$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-axial-terminal-error}
 \|x\|\le r:=2a/\theta,\qquad
 \|x-y\|\le R:=C_G a^2/\theta.
\end{equation}
To check this, the local inverse of the exponential has norm at
most twice the chord and differs from curvature by at most a fixed
constant times chord squared. Both statements follow from their
common derivative at the identity and Taylor's formula on a fixed
small ball. For a product of $l\le4b$ plaquettes, subtract its
curvature from the sum of the individual curvatures successively.
The product estimate of Subsection~\ref{sec:marked-healthy-cumulant-launch}
costs at most $C\sum_{j<l}j\tau^2\le C l^2\tau^2$.
Add the logarithm--curvature error of the product and divide by
$\theta$. This proves \eqref{eq:rank-axial-terminal-error}.

Define $W_u$ by substituting those linear axial sums, multiplied by
$g/\theta$, into $L_u$, where $g=\beta_{N,p,k}^{-1/2}$.
Evaluating $W_u$ at the based plaquette curvatures divided by $g$
gives exactly $L_u(y)$: the factors $g$ cancel, with no approximation.
This is also the radial conversion in the pre-pin construction;
on a radial test its linear axial sums are the ordinary radial
link integrals. Let $F_u^{\rm near}$ be the unmasked near-list sum.
If $r\le\rho_G$ and $r+R\le1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-raw-terminal-ticket}
 |F_u^{\rm near}(U)-W_u(g^{-1}\mathcal C(U))|
 \le4M_u r^{D+1}+\eta M_u+D|L_u|_1R.
\end{equation}
Indeed the gauge-fixed field and $U(x)$ agree on all near supports,
so their near sums agree by locality and gauge invariance. At $U(x)$
the full list is $F_u$, its wide sum is at most $\eta M_u$ in
absolute value, and its Taylor error is bounded by
\eqref{eq:rank-list-taylor-error}. Finally, on the coordinate unit
ball, replacing the factors of each degree-$j$ monomial one at a
time bounds $|L_u(x)-L_u(y)|$ by $D|L_u|_1R$.
Every step uses the same field and the same raw polynomial.
Its physical evaluation is gauge invariant because all based
curvatures rotate by the gauge at the anchor and $W_u$ is invariant.
It is measurable and local on the patch, being a polynomial in
continuous based curvature coordinates. Enlarging to a halo of radius
$qB$ with $b=8B$ and $q\ge8$ preserves these properties and the
healthy-field estimate.

For the selected thresholds write $s=\widehat\beta_k$,
$H=\exp((\log\log s)^2)$ and
\[
 \theta=s^{-1/2}H,\qquad
 \tau=s^{-1/2}\exp((\log\log s)^{3/2}).
\]
The patch radius $b=8\lceil\log^2s\rceil^2$ grows more slowly than
every positive power of $H$. Thus for every $\epsilon>0$,
$r\le H^{-1+\epsilon}$ eventually. Also $R$ and
$\eta=\exp(-e\lceil\log^2s\rceil)$, with $e$ bounded below by a
fixed positive constant, are smaller than every negative power of
$H$. Suppose the column and dimension prices have been paid so that,
with $\gamma=1/(16(D+2))$,
\[
 M_u\le H^\gamma|Au|_1,\qquad
 |L_u|_1\le4H^{2\gamma}|Au|_1.
\]
Take $\epsilon=\gamma$. The first term in
\eqref{eq:rank-raw-terminal-ticket}, divided by $|Au|_1$, is at most
$4H^{-D-1+\gamma(D+2)}=4H^{-D-15/16}$.
The other terms are smaller than $H^{-D}$ by an arbitrarily large
power. Consequently, after one uniform increase of the threshold,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-raw-terminal-paid}
 |F_u^{\rm near}(U)-W_u(g^{-1}\mathcal C(U))|
                         \le H^{-D}|Au|_1.
\end{equation}
For a solved lift the bound in
Subsection~\ref{sec:allarity-coordinate-calibration} gives
$|Au|_1\le H^{bd+q_*+2}$ eventually, on a fixed bounded target set.
Thus any fixed $D>bd+q_*+2+v$ makes this error at most $H^{-v}$.
Here the amplification exponent $b$ is the one in the calibration
equation, not the patch radius. It is chosen before $D$ and before
the stage's derivative order. For the raw coefficient bound, each axial
row has coefficient sum at most $4b\,g/\theta$. Here
$g/\theta=H^{-1}\sqrt{s/\beta_{N,p,k}}\le\sqrt{8/7}\,H^{-1}$.
Substitution into a degree-$D$ polynomial costs at most
$\max\{1,4b\,g/\theta\}^D$, which is eventually one. Alternatively
keeping $(4b)^D$ as a price costs only $H^{o(1)}$. Thus the same
column and dimension purchases give $|W_u|_1\le H|Au|_1$. These are the local value and coefficient bounds
needed for a terminal stamp; they are not a bound for evaluating
$\mathcal H u$ on the physical field.

\subparagraph{The terminal mask correction and the lift's price.}
Let $f_u$ be the near sum with each original support mask restored
except for the scalar unit, which remains unmasked,
and let $X$ be the terminal halo containing all those supports and
all raw polynomial reads. Put $\chi=\mathbf1_{\mathrm{Healthy}(\tau,X)}$.
Since $\tau\le\theta$ eventually, $f_u=F_u^{\rm near}$ wherever
$\chi=1$. The near-list prices give $|f_u|\le3M_u$ on every field:
each masked regular term is bounded by its own zeroth-order price.
There is the exact decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-terminal-three-pieces}
 f_u=\chi W_u+\chi(F_u^{\rm near}-W_u)
                         +(f_u-\chi F_u^{\rm near}).
\end{equation}
The middle term is a supported insertion with value at most
$H^{-D}|Au|_1$ on its healthy domain; this assertion supplies a value price, not a
derivative price. The last term vanishes on
that domain and has value at most $3M_u$ everywhere else. It is
therefore a forced insertion, with the halo enlarged to include
all flag tests of $X$. No bound for the unmasked polynomial on an
unhealthy field has been used: in that region both terms containing
$\chi$ vanish. The wide terms keep their original masks and
exponential support prices; they were not replaced by a polynomial
on a large wrapping support.

For an external marker weight $\rho\ge0$, these two new prices
are respectively $\rho H^{-D}|Au|_1$ and $3\rho M_u$.
If $C_k$ is the column price, $M_u=C_k|Au|_1$, and
$C_k\le H$, the solved-lift estimate above gives
\[
 |W_u|_1\le H^E,\quad M_u\le H^E,
 \qquad E=bd+q_*+3,
\]
eventually for a fixed bounded set of targets; enlarge the threshold
to absorb their fixed constants. Choosing $\rho=H^{-E-9}$ gives
$3\rho M_u\le H^{-8}$. Choosing the Taylor order $D>E+v$
gives the required value error at most $H^{-v}$ before weighting.
All choices are made before the final stage threshold. The geometric
and degree prices of the marked compiler must still be applied to
these same supports and pieces; the value estimate is not a substitute
for those separate compiler hypotheses.

\subparagraph{Attaching the lists to the selected coefficient.}
Here are the precise data furnished by the calibrated family used at the
start of this section. At $(k,N,p)$ let $x_u$ be the coordinate of its
actual coefficient and put $r_k=(a_k^{\rm mesh})^{-d}$, so that
$Au=r_kx_u$. Write $C_k\ge1$ for the column count and $\theta$ for
its threshold. Its regular remainder is a countable family
$(X_a,h_a)$, with nonnegative direction prices $e_a$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-selected-cap}
 \sum_a e^{e_k q_a}e_a\le C_k|x_u^\circ|_1,
 \qquad q_a=\operatorname{cov}_{M_k}(X_a),
 \qquad M_k=\lceil(\log s)^2\rceil.
\end{equation}
The supports are connected and contain the anchor; the remainder has
zero local reading. These are the format and remainder-cap clauses of
the calibrated coefficient, obtained by the finite backward construction
of Subsection~\ref{ex-centered-freight-producer} and the splitting of
Subsection~\ref{sec:local-splitting}. In particular this cap is for the
coefficient of the selected family, not a separately normalized list.
As throughout this section, the funded calibrated-family input is retained;
we are spelling out its use, not constructing a new family.

Let $S_\alpha$ be the fixed local section for a nonconstant frame axis,
and let $r_\alpha$ count its curvature factors. Its purchased direction
price is $C_k\theta^{r_\alpha}$. Its coefficient in the original
exponential entry is $\theta^{-r_\alpha}(x_u)_\alpha$.
Consequently its price after multiplication by $r_k$ is exactly
$r_k C_k|(x_u)_\alpha|$. The unit contributes $r_k|(x_u)_0|$,
with no support mask. Their total is at most $2C_k|Au|_1$.
Multiplying \eqref{eq:rank-selected-cap} by $r_k$ gives at most
$C_k|Au|_1$ for all the regular terms. Split this very list at
$q_a\le M_k$. The near and wide prices are therefore
$3C_k|Au|_1$ and $e^{-e_kM_k}C_k|Au|_1$, respectively.
Thus \eqref{eq:rank-list-input} holds with
\[
 M_u=C_k|Au|_1,\qquad \eta=e^{-e_kM_k}.
\]
No lower bound on $|Au|_1$ is needed: if it vanishes, all these
nonnegative prices vanish.

The original covering radius in this calculation is $M_k$. The
auxiliary terminal covering radius is $B_k=M_k^2$. They must not be
interchanged. A connected support containing the anchor with covering
number $q_a$ at radius $M_k$ has distance from that anchor at most
$q_a(4M_k+2)+2M_k$: order a connected chain of covering boxes from
an anchor box to the point's box, paying at most $4M_k+2$ for each
link and $2M_k$ for the endpoint boxes. A chain uses at most $q_a$
boxes, after deleting loops. For a near support this is at most
$4M_k^2+4M_k\le8M_k^2$. The fixed local-section stencil also lies
in this patch eventually. Hence all near supports lie in the very
patch $b=8B_k$ used for $W_u$. The purchased room gives $10b$ less
than the torus side, so these supports are nonwrapping, and a
terminal halo of radius $qB_k$, $q\ge8$, contains them and all
axial reads. This checks the support, projection and locality
conditions of the Taylor construction.

\subparagraph{Paying the order and columns on one calendar.}
Choose $D$ before the stage profile, large enough for the fixed finite
grade menu, the solved-lift amplification and the requested terminal
error. Include $D+1$ in the stage derivative-order request. The column
count $c_j$ of stage $j$ is fixed after these order requests and before
its block length; at level $k$ it is $C_k=c_{J(k)}$. Put
$\gamma=1/(16(D+2))$. For fixed $c_j$ there is a finite threshold
$s_j$ such that
\[
 c_j\le \exp\{\gamma(\log\log s)^2\}\quad(s\ge s_j).
\]
This follows directly by taking logarithms; it is enough that
$\log\log s\ge\sqrt{\log c_j/\gamma}$. Include $s_j$ among the
post-length delays in the purchase at the beginning of this section.
It is independent of $N,p,u$. For a stage with several requests take
the largest threshold and derivative order; the menu is finite.
The all-stage payment then gives $C_k\le H^\gamma$ at every admitted
level, not merely on a subsequence. Increasing delays does not change
already selected profiles or columns. This is the exact column purchase
needed above; the weaker $C_k\le H$ in the forced-price calculation
is an immediate consequence.

There are at most $c_G(2b+1)^4$ real link coordinates in the patch,
uniformly in the period. Since $b=8M_k^2$, its logarithm is
$O(\log\log s)$, whereas $\log H=(\log\log s)^2$.
Thus $C_D(1+n_b)^D\le H^\gamma$ eventually. The Taylor estimate
now gives both $M_u\le H^\gamma|Au|_1$ and
$|L_u|_1\le4H^{2\gamma}|Au|_1$. The same argument absorbs every
fixed power of $b$ in the ray and raw-coefficient estimates.
The positive support exponent can be kept bounded below, for example
by one, in the finite stage requests. Its wide price is then smaller
than every power of $H^{-1}$. All these delays precede the selection
of the finite-volume endpoint. They prove the quantitative hypotheses
of the pre-pin and terminal estimates on that endpoint simultaneously.

\subparagraph{Which part of the birth is replaced.}
Write $h_u^{\rm wide}$ for the wide sum with its original masks.
The selected coefficient's exponential entry, in physical normalization,
is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-selected-entry-split}
 F_u^{\rm exp}=f_u+h_u^{\rm wide}.
\end{equation}
Indeed its definition is the global scalar, the masked local section
and the masked regular series. Expand the local section in its frame
axes and split the regular series at $q_a\le M_k$. The absolute
majorant in \eqref{eq:rank-selected-cap} permits this partition on
every configuration, including configurations on mask boundaries.
This proves \eqref{eq:rank-selected-entry-split} with precisely the
lists used to construct $L_u$ and $W_u$.

The actual coefficient density also has its original forced terms;
\eqref{eq:rank-selected-entry-split} does not discard them. Replace
only $f_u$ by \eqref{eq:rank-terminal-three-pieces}. Keep
$h_u^{\rm wide}$ and all original forced terms at their original
addresses. This is an exact equality of the single-mark density on
the same configuration and background representation. For several
marks the terminal expression is the finite squarefree exponential
of these marked contributions, together with their prescribed
background factors. Substitution of the single-mark equality in
each factor preserves every coefficient: expand the finite exponential,
and distribute each chosen singleton among its three replacement
terms. The wide and original forced factors are unchanged. Absolute
support prices justify any countable regular sums before this finite
algebraic operation. Translations, signed coordinate changes and
conjugation preserve the identity by pullback. The new principal
singleton is $\chi W_u$; its supported error and mask correction
are exactly the other two terms of
\eqref{eq:rank-terminal-three-pieces}, with the prices already proved.
In particular the raw polynomial is not asserted to equal the full
birth coefficient, nor to equal its near part off the healthy set.

 k-w\le2+\frac{(\bar b+2)y^2+10\log c_k}{\log3}\le y^4

\begin{proposition}[The output representation meets the ticket suppositions]
\label{prop:rank-trace-output-lawful}
Assume the conclusions of Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-trace-chart-window}(1).
Assume also that the auxiliary representation $P_w$ at the root depth $w$
carries a threshold with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-trace-threshold-window}
 (8/7)^{1/2}\,\widehat\beta_w^{-1/2}\le\theta_{P_w}\le\theta_{\rm clock}(w),
\end{equation}
and that the stage profiles have clip constants with
$0<c_{\rm lin}<c_{\rm dir}/16$. Assume finally the following
path-and-clip receipt.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(P)] The coarse path action of $P_w$ in the restarted representation
 is the retained path $R_{N,w}$ of
 Proposition~\ref{prop:path-retained-plan}, evaluated at the constant
 coupling $\beta_{N,p,k}$. At that coupling it satisfies
 $R_{N,w}.\mathrm{Bounds}(p_w,\sigma_w,A_w,\mathrm{loopCap}_w)$, with the
 clip $p_w$ and row price $A_w$ of the stage $\pi_{J(w)}$.
\end{enumerate}
Then, for all sufficiently large $k$,
every chart at level $k$ with base arc $b\le\bar b$, every $N\ge k$ and
every $p$, $P_w$ is a lawful representation satisfying the suppositions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-root-output-ticket}(2) at the output level $w$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item its coupling $\beta=\beta_{N,p,k}$ satisfies $g=\beta^{-1/2}\le\theta_{P_w}\le1$
 and $\beta\le4\widehat\beta_w$;
\item $\theta_{P_w}\le\theta_{\rm clock}(w)$;
\item its loop budget satisfies $\Lambda\le\Lambda_{\rm clock}(w)$, and its
 clip and row bound are those of the stage $\pi_{J(w)}$;
\item its additive terms have value bound $\delta\le1$ per site.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Lawfulness.} By Subsection~\ref{sec:restarted-rank-history}, the
auxiliary representation has gauge-invariant, local and motion-covariant
path action, additive terms and activities. Its density is the push
\eqref{eq:restart-push} of the nonnegative density above it along a
positive kernel, so it is nonnegative. The clip constants are those of the
profile.

\emph{Coupling.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-trace-endpoint} and the plateau,
$\beta\ge\tfrac78\widehat\beta_k\ge\tfrac78\widehat\beta_w$, so
$g\le(8/7)^{1/2}\widehat\beta_w^{-1/2}\le\theta_{P_w}$ by
\eqref{eq:rank-trace-threshold-window}. Also
$\theta_{P_w}\le\theta_{\rm clock}(w)\le1$ for large $\widehat\beta_w$, and
$\beta\le4\widehat\beta_w$ is part of
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-trace-chart-window}(1). Since $w(k)\to\infty$,
these thresholds are reached for all large $k$.

\emph{Threshold.} This is the upper bound in
\eqref{eq:rank-trace-threshold-window}.

\emph{Paths.} By (P), the coarse path action of $P_w$ is $R_{N,w}$ at the
coupling $\beta_{N,p,k}$, and it satisfies the bounds of
Definition~\ref{def:path-admissible} there. Its clip is $p_w$, its row
price is $A_w$, and its loop budget is at most
$\mathrm{loopCap}_w=2\mathcal M_w^{\theta_{J(w)}}=\Lambda_{\rm clock}(w)$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:common-loop-capacity} and
Subsection~\ref{subsec:rank-root-clock-threshold}, with
$\vartheta_j=\theta_j$.

\emph{Additive terms.} By \eqref{eq:restart-anchored}, summing the
anchored bounds gives $\sum_\alpha|E_\alpha(U)|\le|\Lambda|q_n$, so the
value bound per site is at most $q_n\le s^{-1/400}\le1$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-trace-chart-window}(1).

Two thresholds satisfy \eqref{eq:rank-trace-threshold-window} for all
large $k$. The first is the threshold prescribed in (F3),
$\widehat\beta_w^{-1/2}\exp((\log\log\widehat\beta_w)^{3/2})$, since
$\log\log\widehat\beta_w\ge1$ on this clock and the exponential tends

The path and clip assertions in (P) follow from the restart construction

\begin{lemma}[The path part of the receipt (P)]
\label{lem:rank-trace-path-part}
Suppose that at every step of the window $[w,k]$ the restart construction
is applied as in Subsection~\ref{sec:actual-local-compiler-handoff}, with the
same-course conditions stated there. Then the coarse path action of $P_w$
in the restarted representation is the retained path $R_{N,w}$, and its
clip is $p_w$. Moreover
$R_{N,w}.\mathrm{Bounds}(p_w,\sigma_w,A_w,\mathrm{loopCap}_w)$ holds, with
the clip $p_w$ and the row price $A_w$ of the stage $\pi_{J(w)}$, whatever
the coupling of the representation. Hence the path-and-clip receipt (P) holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The selected history carries the selected paths $R_{N,j}$ and the stage
clips $p_j$ at every level $j$ (Subsection~\ref{sec:common-funded-history}). At
$k$ the restarted history uses the endpoint re-expression of
Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-endpoint}, which preserves the selected
path and clip while changing the budget and keeping the density. Each restart step sets the new coarse coupling equal to the
incoming one and preserves the original coarse path and clip, that is,
those of the same step of the selected history
(Subsection~\ref{sec:actual-local-compiler-handoff}). By induction down the
window, the restarted representation at every level $j\in[w,k]$ carries
$R_{N,j}$ and $p_j$. A path is a collection of row, loop, cubic and
quartic coefficients, and the predicate $\mathrm{Bounds}$ concerns only
these coefficients: weighted sums, invariance, the metric identity, and
the summability and vanishing of the loop moments. It does not involve the
coupling or the threshold. By Proposition~\ref{prop:path-retained-plan},
$R_{N,w}$ is admissible at $(N,w)$, so by
Definition~\ref{def:path-admissible} it satisfies the displayed bounds.
\end{proof}

The phrase ``evaluated at the constant coupling'' in (P) therefore adds
nothing to the path bounds: the coupling enters the density, not the path
coefficients. The same-course conditions are the local hypotheses of the
restarted step, which the input (V) also concerns.

\begin{corollary}[All-level lawful history for the trace chart]
\label{cor:rank-trace-all-levels}
Fix a finite grade menu and $\bar b>0$. Keep the funded chart/stamp
hypotheses and finite adjacent-pair purchases of Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-actual-closure}, together
with the selected path bounds and the stage clip constants
$0<c_{\rm lin}<c_{\rm dir}/16$. Then the family of
Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-chart-family} supplies (F3) of
Subsection~\ref{subsec:ex-lattice-firesky-witness} at every sufficiently
large level $k$, for every chart with arc $0<b\le\bar b$.
The assertion holds simultaneously for every
$j\in[w,k]$, every $N>k$ and every period $p$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the constructed $P_j$ themselves throughout. Their budget radius is
$\lceil(\log\widehat\beta_j)^2\rceil^2$ and their threshold is exactly
$\tau_j=\widehat\beta_j^{-1/2}
\exp((\log\log\widehat\beta_j)^{3/2})$, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:restart-chart-family}. The same proposition supplies
the density identities, nonnegativity, local format, and unchanged
path and clip at every level, including the re-expressed top $k$.
The path bounds of Definition~\ref{def:path-admissible} concern the
coefficients and are independent of the coupling. Thus the argument of
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-trace-path-part} applies to every $j$.

Here are all the numerical tests for the normalization estimate.
Set $\beta=\beta_{N,p,k}$ and $g=\beta^{-1/2}$. The endpoint running
estimate and the plateau give, for every $j\in[w,k]$,
\[
 \tfrac78\widehat\beta_k\le\beta\le\tfrac98\widehat\beta_k,
 \qquad \tfrac12\widehat\beta_k\le\widehat\beta_j
                        \le\widehat\beta_k.
\]
Consequently $\beta>0$, $\beta g^2=1$,
$\beta\le\tfrac94\widehat\beta_j\le4\widehat\beta_j$ and
$g\le\sqrt{8/7}\,\widehat\beta_j^{-1/2}$. The factor
$\exp((\log\log\widehat\beta_j)^{3/2})$ eventually exceeds
$\sqrt{8/7}$, so $g\le\tau_j$.
For $\log\log\widehat\beta_j\ge1$, its exponent is no larger than
$(\log\log\widehat\beta_j)^2$; hence
$\tau_j\le\theta_{\rm clock}(j)\le1$ on the purchased tail.
The clip is exactly the stage clip, not merely bounded by one.
Its row bound is $A_j$ and its loop allowance is bounded by
$\mathrm{loopCap}_j=\Lambda_{\rm clock}(j)$, by the selected path
bounds and Lemma~\ref{lem:common-loop-capacity}.
Finally the additive value cost is at most
$q_{k-j}\le s^{-1/400}\le1$, where $s=\widehat\beta_k$.
These are the clip, coupling, entrance, upper-threshold, additive and
loop tests used in Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-root-output-ticket}(2).
They leave no separate test--threshold assumption.

The costs are uniform for $0\le k-j\le k-w$, and the plateau makes
