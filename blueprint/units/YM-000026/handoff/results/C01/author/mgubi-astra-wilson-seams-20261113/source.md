## Spatial weights: exact difference between the two presentations

Let V(X) be the source spatial potential and e(X)=exp(−beta V(X)/2). The manuscript's H(X,Y) integrates only the temporal plaquette weight. The source defines `weight(X,Y,u)=e(X) exp(−beta transition(X,Y,u)) e(Y)`, so its `heat(X,Y)=e(X) H(X,Y) e(Y)`. The spatial potential is gauge invariant, hence these factors are unaffected by the common-thread average. The source's circular product allocates two half-potentials to each slice; the manuscript allocates the whole spatial potential directly to its half action. Their full unnormalized integrands agree. For site cuts, source area products carry halves of the fixed-plane spatial weights in both factors, while the manuscript keeps the complete fixed-plane weight outside the absolute square. These are equal allocations of the same positive weight; no square root of a probe or missing partition factor is involved.
