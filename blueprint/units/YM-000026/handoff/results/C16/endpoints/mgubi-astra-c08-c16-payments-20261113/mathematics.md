## Exact local outputs and consumers

### Actual marked derivatives

`sec:marked-activity-jets`, especially `eq:marked-held-factor`,
`eq:marked-pointwise-jets`, `eq:marked-integral-jets` and
`eq:marked-local-log-term`, supplies finite-order derivatives of the local
extension on a neighborhood of a healthy base point, under the compensated
transport, density, covariance, cutoff and Gaussian moment hypotheses. The
bounded measurable insertion is held fixed; it is not differentiated. The
mask is not differentiated either. An extension and its masked contribution
are not asserted equal on arbitrary unhealthy configurations.

`sec:leaf-expansion` then uses a summable joint value/derivative bound on an
open tilt interval. Its product estimate controls the pointed series before
the marked index is summed. `eq:leaf-gas-derivative` therefore uses the actual
entrywise derivative k_j at each original support/inventory address. The
logarithmic derivative and the tilted identity retain the factor a(theta)/a(0).
The exact normalization is the same-data identity proved in
`eq:marked-coarse-normalization`; numerical smallness alone cannot replace it.
`eq:leaf-tail-power` gives the nonprincipal tail at fine exponent q/4<0.

C16's `sec:nonlinear-payment-selection` buys the finitely many fixed-order
bounds after the block and transport radius are chosen; its final paragraph
explicitly leaves the funded Gaussian data, coefficient and normalization
identities as separate steps. C16's `sec:ope-history-service` uses that very
forced derivative, not an arbitrary array having the same evaluated gas.

### Two distinct soft corrections

`eq:nonlinear-payment` is uniform in all nonnegative hard/soft inputs a,b
below the stated bounds, once C,D0,f and the block are fixed. The first
absorption has hard input exp(-mu P_f^2/4) and soft input of negative upper
exponent (including a fixed enlargement factor in the multiple-insertion
version). It yields 2 exp(-mu P_f^2/8). The second has this first output as
hard input and the separately bounded reset/path soft price; it yields
2 exp(-mu P_f^2/16). These are `eq:nonlinear-two-payments`.

The soft reset/path estimate `eq:nonlinear-soft-price` uses |alpha|<=A,
0<=H<=t^2 and 0<t<=1, with fixed positive widths and derivative orders.
The radius-one constants are selected separately for the two correction
families/reserves. Both fit C_A(1+B)^4 t eventually, but equality of their
analytic arrays or costs is neither needed nor asserted. In the source the
first and second activity families are literally `shifting` and `pupil`,
with `indoors` and `brisk` using different payments; the latter's hard input
is the former's allowance. The two regrouping identities are separately
`curd` and `cord`. This matches C08's two fixed logarithmic corrections R,S
in the normalization argument. A single absorption would not supply the
quoted final forced price.

### Retained profile and degree-zero transport

`eq:projection-singleton-entrywise` defines D(M) as the actual derivative
of the twice-regrouped forced coefficient F^M. The separate identity
`eq:projection-profile-retained` is

    P(Q_forced) - hook(psi) = L^d [P(D(M1)) + i P(D(M2))].

The minus sign removes the hook added by local splitting. This is a
coefficient-profile equality, stronger than equality of integrated densities.
Absolute forced-price domination justifies the profile sums and affordable
linear combinations; no arbitrary reindexing of inventories is allowed.
C16 explicitly retains this D and these original addresses in H2/H3.

For H2, the degree-zero small bills imply 2 p_M<=m and 2 q_M<=m:
the actual second bill is 2 kappa^(-1) q_M<=c0 m, with 0<kappa<=1 and
c0<=1. Thus q_M is the unscaled forced derivative bill; silently using the
scaled bill in the profile would be wrong. The geometric floor E_*<=L
is chosen before L. Compression gives
2(p_M+E_*m)+2q_M <= (2+2L)m <= 6Lm.
The principal term is absent for flagged input. For ordinary input its
masked value is bounded by the insertion height and its support enlargement
cost is E_*. Finite transport preserves the actual profile and density;
constants are preserved using T rho_f=rho_c. Lawful slots, integrability and
the same kernel/representations are still hypotheses.

### Projection allowance and occupancy prices

`eq:projection-large-allowance` bounds the splitting bill Q_d(x) by
exp(sqrt(y)), where y=betaFine(x). The principal factor has finite upper
exponent, so the exponential dominates it at fixed stage, L and d.
The tail and forced bills are paid separately, including the inverse square
root of the **coarse** forced allowance. These are not absorbed merely by
increasing the final projection allowance.

For `eq:projection-occupancy-payments`, C16 substitutes exactly

    x=betaHat_m, y=betaHat_(m+1), L=L_{J(m+1)},
    N=N_{J(m+1)}, B=B_m, alpha=100+1/2,
    eta=y^(-2), tau=chi_m=2 exp(-mu P(y)^2/16).

The producer's s(x) is C16's `eq:ope-history-cluster-price`:
2(1+N) eta (N exp(alpha)+1)+(1+N) tau. In particular every contribution
retains 1+N; eta is the single-insertion allowance y^-2, distinct from the
ordinary tariff y^-1. The four purchased inequalities give
(2B+1)^4 s<=1/2, exp(s)-1<=1, tau<=1 and
alpha(2048B+1)^4<=sqrt(y), exactly
`eq:ope-history-smallness` and `eq:ope-history-support-cost`.
The same y gives X_m=exp(sqrt(y)). The stage profiles are exactly 100;
the larger local-function budget being at least 100 is a different fact.

These are numerical payments for a **given** occupancy bound N. They do not
prove that an arbitrary marker family meets that occupancy or its offer
bound. The product-set marker hypotheses remain with the C02/menu caller;
that separate endpoint is outside this task.

## Quantifiers and retained data

For incoming stage j=J(m+1), the outgoing stage i=J(m) is j or j-1.
Pre-length floors include the fixed footprint conversion and E_*<=L.
After lengths, prices, protection and the occupancy schedule are fixed,
take finite maxima over permitted i and degrees d<=i. Increase the entry
delays, retaining their conclusions under every later increase. There is no
single maximum over all stages, degrees, insertions or cutoffs.
`eq:nonlinear-entry-max` and the final projection-payment paragraphs state
this order; `common-observable-purchases` imports the same bounds and N_j.
The local thresholds are uniform over permitted insertions and finite tori;
the unweighted sum used in differentiation can depend on the fixed volume.

For m>=t_d with d+3<=J(t_d), we have d<=J(m), so the degree-d payment
is available on every permitted transition there. Its local constants do
not depend on the later cutoff N0>m or period. Applying it to the selected
history still requires the exact identities matching its local input/output
representations, interval point, kernel, clipping, path, budgets and forced
dictionary to the retained witnesses. `sec:public-run-binding` retains R,T,W
and does not select a new history just to satisfy one payment.
