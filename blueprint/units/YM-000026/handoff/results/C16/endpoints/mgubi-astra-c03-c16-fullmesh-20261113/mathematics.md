## The producer is a full ultraviolet result

`thm:ex-fullmesh-fixedbounds-producer` gives, for each finite label list,
finite difference list and positive physical separation r, a finite bound
and one mesh threshold valid **for every period**. The mesh-period preorder
compares mesh indices only. It is not the product order and does not discard
small fixed periods. Site coordinates are integers; physical positions are
a_N times those coordinates, and each difference is divided by a_N.
The separation is the periodic maximum-coordinate distance, with physical
factor a_N. These conventions agree with the adjacent Cauchy argument.

The theorem explicitly assumes the paid coefficient-row/primary trial and
zero-projection sweep data, exact difference identity, planted product
inequality, and budgets for every signed-coordinate/conjugated orientation
used by the finite list. For each oriented row family there is a depth k0;
for every centered k>=k0 there is a budget and a period-uniform mesh tail.
The proof chooses the coarse separation scale first, then a sufficiently
fine centered scale beyond all the finitely many k0, then the common mesh
tail. Translation invariance alone does not supply the fine-to-coarse root
comparison or the orientation budgets. The #157 repair is retained.

`sec:fullmesh-correlation-cauchy` fixes a period, finite insertions and one
history across all terminal cutoffs. Its representations, transfers, local
fields, masks and Wilson partition functions are those of that family.
The higher-marker omission bound `eq:fullmesh-omission` is
2 C_r exp(-B_j/64), where the root r is fixed before the later rung j and
C_r does not grow with j or the sufficiently large cutoff. At fixed j, the
erased numerator is uniformly Cauchy and the stripped partition function is
Cauchy with a positive lower bound. Choosing j first and then the cutoff
tail proves Cauchy convergence of the original correlation along N itself.
It is not a Cauchy claim only along a newly selected subsequence.

Ancestral points are dense. The schedule condition a_m B_m^2 -> 0 gives the
required later-rung separation for each fixed distinct periodic configuration.
Together with the finite-difference bounds, `ex-smooth-grid-limit` yields a
smooth fixed-period kernel on NoHit_p and locally uniform convergence of
every **fixed** finite difference request, with its own thresholds. NoHit_p
allows coordinate ties and seam-crossing neighborhoods, but excludes actual
collisions modulo the period. The theorem does not cover derivative orders,
labels or stencils drifting with N. The cell-integral argument then gives
full-mesh smeared limits for each suitable compactly supported test.

The strong period-uniform bound and the eventual mesh convergence have
different quantifiers: the latter may have a threshold depending on the
fixed period, labels, compact set, derivative request and test. The endpoint
requires the limit at each suitable fixed period, not one convergence tail
uniform over all periods and tests.

## Exact match to the extracted rows

`rem:ex-limits-fullmesh` correctly retains the extraction route: a hierarchy
is still extracted on a refined order; full mesh convergence is established
separately and then identified. Its optional direct-correlation route does
not transfer all hierarchy data to the full mesh order.

For a fixed test and labels, C16's `ex-full-mesh` assumes the same finite
correlations W_(N,p) have full row limits ell_p and extracted row limits w_p
along one strictly increasing mesh map u. Strict increase implies u(j)->infinity,
so the full limit also holds along u. Uniqueness gives ell_p=w_p at each
suitable period. This uses the **same** W, hence the same finite-volume law,
normalization, labels, test and family. There is no identification between
limits of two separately chosen Wilson families.

The extracted volume limit is w_(p(q(k)))->s. Eventual suitability makes
ell_(p(q(k))) and w_(p(q(k))) equal on a tail, so ell has that same volume
limit s. Suitability is not required at every initial period. Compact test
support and diverging periods supply eventual suitability, and every further
strictly increasing period subsequence preserves it.

This proves only the limit-identification implication. It does not infer
full mesh convergence from extraction, assert a full volume limit, or replace
an iterated limit by a diagonal (N_k,p_k) limit. The same row function can be
chosen arbitrarily at unsuitable periods since these do not enter the row
assertion and disappear from the eventual volume tail.

## Second refinement and the retained family

`sec:public-run-binding` retains R,T,W and the literal family C_R. For initial
order f, the first refinement g supplies coupling limits and the second w
supplies field/hierarchy limits; the combined order is (f refine g) refine w,
equivalently f refine (g refine w). Coupling limits persist by composition
with the strict subsequences. The period suitability tail persists as well.
The full ultraviolet result is independently available on C_R and can be
identified with the final hierarchy's row limits at this combined order.

In the source this is exactly `fromFunded`: the coupling limits are passed
through `Order.retained`, and `from_spec` is then applied to the final
Spec.Final and the separate fullMesh receipt for R.family. It preserves the
chosen table and the volume refinement. It does **not** assert that CellHyp,
calibrated field data or every part of Spec.Final holds on the full mesh order.
C16's `ex-joint-input` item 1 asks for the full-mesh mixed-correlation and
refined-volume limits, while keeping the rest of the hierarchy in the same
joint construction. Its final assembly remains conditional.
