## Mathematics

I rederived each step independently before comparing it with the text.

1. **Copy geometry (eq. 103).** For the link case (δ = 1) the reversed relative coordinate is `ℓ−1−r`, which maps `[0, ℓ)` onto itself. For the site case (δ = 0) it is `ℓ−r`, which maps `(0, ℓ)` onto itself. `φ_v` is an affine signed map, so it sends nearest-neighbour links to nearest-neighbour links, possibly reversed. Copies of home-chamber observables are therefore local to the image chamber. Pullbacks along `φ_v` are measurable, transport gauge transformations, and preserve sup bounds. `ℓ v_i` is well defined mod `2hℓ` because `v_i` is defined mod `2h`. Parity is well defined mod `2h` because `2h` is even. Correct.
2. **Mate identity `φ_{mv} = r_{p,a} ∘ φ_v`.** I checked both parity cases with `c = b_a + ℓ p_a`.
   - `v_a` even: `r(φ_v(x))_a = 2c − δ − b_a − ℓ v_a − (x_a − b_a) = b_a + ℓ(2p_a − v_a) − δ − (x_a − b_a)`. This equals `φ_{mv}(x)_a`, because `(mv)_a = 2p_a − 1 − v_a` is odd and `ℓ(2p_a−1−v_a) + ℓ = ℓ(2p_a − v_a)`.
   - `v_a` odd: `r(φ_v(x))_a = b_a + ℓ(2p_a − 1 − v_a) + (x_a − b_a) = φ_{mv}(x)_a`, with `(mv)_a` even.

   Off-axis coordinates are unchanged. Pullbacks compose contravariantly, `P_{r∘φ} = P_φ ∘ P_r`, so `F ∘ P_{φ_{mv}} = (F ∘ P_{φ_v}) ∘ R_{p,a}`. Exactly one parity changes, so the conjugation bit flips, which gives (107). Correct, including the remark that only one extra conjugation appears.
3. **Half-torus inclusion.** For a cell with residue `t ∈ [0, h)`, the relative `a`-coordinate is `ℓt + r_a`. This lies in `[0, hℓ)` (link) or `(0, hℓ)` (site). The reflection `y ↦ −δ − y` maps the stated positive half exactly onto its complement (link) or onto its complement minus the two fixed planes (site). Products of copies from `S` are therefore admissible in (104). Correct.
4. **Form and Cauchy–Schwarz.** `B(P, Q) = E[P · conj(Q∘R)]`. Invariance gives `E[conj(Q) (P∘R)] = E[conj(Q∘R) P]`, using `R² = 1`, so `B` is Hermitian. Expanding `B(P − tQ, P − tQ)` gives exactly `u − t̄z − t z̄ + |t|²v`. The choice `t = z/v` when `v > 0` gives `|z|² ≤ uv`. When `v = 0` and `z ≠ 0`, `t = Rz` gives `u − 2R|z|² < 0` for large `R`. The test functions `P − tQ` stay admissible, being linear combinations of products of copies on the positive half. Correct. This avoids the non-degeneracy assumption (`E(f ϑ f) = 0 ⇒ f = 0`) that Biskup's proof of Theorem 5.8 imposes (arXiv v2, p. 45).
5. **Fold identity and bound (108).** Reindexing `v ∉ S` as `v = mw` with `w ∈ S` (m is a bijection between `S` and its complement) and applying (107) gives `∏_v [A,D](v)^{(v)} = P_A · conj(P_D ∘ R)`. So `E` of the product is `B(P_A, P_D)`. Also `B(P_A, P_A) ≥ 0` equals `V([A,A])`. Correct.
6. **Maximiser closure.** With `D = A∘m`, `[A, D] = A`. When `K > 0`, `K² ≤ V([A,A]) V([D,D])` with both factors in `[0, K]` forces both to equal `K`. When `K = 0` the claim is trivial. Correct. Note that `[D,D]` is the fold that keeps the other half of `A`. The argument needs only `[A,A]`, which is what the spreading step uses.
7. **Spreading.** Folding `[A,A]` at seam `p_a` keeps residues `0, …, h−1` relative to `p_a` and sends residue `2h−1` (cell `p_a − 1`) to residue `0`. A strip of length `k < h` starting at `p_a` therefore becomes a strip of length `k+1` starting at `p_a − 1`. The kept slices need `k ≤ h`, which holds. After `h−1` steps the strip has length `h`. One more fold at its start makes every slice equal to a slice in the strip. Off-axis coordinates are untouched by `m`, so independence along earlier axes is preserved, as the text argues. The case `h = 1` needs only the final fold. Four axes give independence of every coordinate, hence a constant assignment. No power of two is needed. Correct, and complete as written (the "repeat reflections" step is fully explained).
8. **ε-normalisation.** Positive real scalars pass through copies, including conjugated ones. The constant assignment with `X_v` has value `ρ(F_v)^M / r_v^M ≤ 1`, so `K ≤ 1`. Pulling out `∏ r_v` gives `V((F_v)) ≤ ∏(ρ(F_v) + ε)`. Letting `ε ↓ 0` gives (105), with no division by a vanishing root. Correct.
9. **Planted form (106).** `ρ(1) = |E_μ 1|^{1/M} = 1` by normalisation, and every copy of `1` is `1`. Filling unoccupied cells with `1` gives (106). The empty family gives `1 ≤ 1`. Correct.
10. **Attachment.** With `μ` the normalised Wilson law `E_{N,p}` and `𝒯` a centred tiling, (105) is literally the hypothesis (109) of `lem:ex-centered-root-seminorm` (same copy convention, same admissible class). In Theorem `ex-centered-producers`, the planted inequality is no longer assumed directly. It is now derived from the proposition's geometric, invariance and seam-positivity hypotheses for each cut tiling. The theorem still separately assumes "every coordinate cut has a positive seam". This is consistent and introduces no circularity. Seam positivity of the Wilson law remains an upstream input, as the text says.

**Literature.** The proposition is the chessboard estimate (Fröhlich–Israel–Lieb–Simon). I checked the cited source in M. Biskup, *Reflection positivity and phase transitions in lattice spin models*, arXiv:math-ph/0610025v2 (27 Apr 2009), §5.3. Definition 5.7 and Theorem 5.8 are on p. 44 and the proof is on pp. 45–46. The author's description of that version is accurate. It uses real B-block functions and reflections through planes of sites, and requires `L/B` even, not a power of two. Its proof also assumes non-degeneracy of the form, and its passage to `d > 1` is only sketched ("proceeding through all directions"). A self-contained complex variant with both seam kinds and zero roots is therefore justified rather than a rederivation of something citable verbatim. The spreading argument in the manuscript plays the role of Biskup's "longest constant block" argument, done axis by axis.

## Findings

No required change.

- **N1 (notation, law in `\mathcal R_{\mathcal T}`).** `\mathcal R_{\mathcal T}` is defined with `\mathbb E_{N,p}`, but the proposition fixes an arbitrary normalised law `μ` and uses `\mathcal R_{\mathcal T}` in (105)–(106). The proof clearly means `|\mathbb E_μ ∏ F^{(C)}|^{1/M}` (for instance `ρ(1) = |\mathbb E_μ 1|^{1/M}`). Suggest one clause in the statement, "with `\mathbb E_{N,p}` replaced by `\mathbb E_μ` in `\mathcal R_{\mathcal T}`", or stating the proposition directly for the normalised Wilson law.
- **N2 (citation locator).** The page range "pp. 50–52 of the author's lecture-note version" points to `math.ucla.edu/~biskup/PDFs/private/...`. That host returned 403 from this environment, so I could not check those page numbers. In the stable arXiv version, arXiv:math-ph/0610025v2, the same Definition 5.7 and Theorem 5.8 are in §5.3, p. 44, with the proof on pp. 45–46. The published version is in *Methods of Contemporary Mathematical Statistical Physics*, Lecture Notes in Math. 1970, Springer, 2009. I did not read that version, so its pages are unverified here. Suggest citing the arXiv v2 or LNM version with matching locators, ideally through `references.bib`, rather than a URL under a `private/` path.
- **N3 (symbol clashes in the proof).** `v` is used both for cell indices and for `B(Q,Q)` in the Cauchy–Schwarz paragraph. `b` is both the base site and an axis in `E` ("for `b ∈ E`"). Both are unambiguous in context but easy to fix, for example with `w = B(Q,Q)` and `j ∈ E`.
- **O1 (optional).** Reflection invariance of the Wilson law under `R_{p,a}` is a source theorem (`YM.Wilson.moment_pull`), so Theorem `ex-centered-producers` could cite it as established instead of listing it among its assumptions. Doing so would need the manuscript's own statement of that symmetry, which I did not locate in this chapter. I leave it to the author.
- **O2 (optional).** The text could add the original reference for the chessboard estimate: J. Fröhlich, R. Israel, E. H. Lieb, B. Simon, *Phase transitions and reflection positivity I*, Commun. Math. Phys. 62 (1978) 1–34. I could not open it from here (publisher hosts blocked), so no theorem locator is verified. It could also say in one sentence why the proof is given in full: complex observables, link seams, and no non-degeneracy assumption.
