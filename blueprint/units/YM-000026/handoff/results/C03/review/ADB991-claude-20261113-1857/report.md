## Findings

**N1 (minor): hypotheses of `ex-phase-original-null`.** Lemma 37.2 sits in Section 37, whose standing hypotheses include (ex-all-copy-bound) and (ex-raw-slot-identity). Proposition 39.5 cites it twice (l.505, l.520) but lists only the Section 33 slab/compression objects and continuity. The lemma's proof uses only bounded slab insertion, the matrix-element identity, density and placement independence, all of which 39.5 assumes. So the use is sound, but one clause should say this, or else add the Section 37 standing hypotheses (in force whenever `ex-phase-caps` supplies the continuity). Step 9 also proves the inclusion directly: take K_{de} replaced by 0 and a ∈ N_d.

**N2 (minor): "smooth" in the cited constructions.** The last paragraph applies `prop:phase-field-inclusions`, `prop:phase-field-union` and the two-table paragraph to S_χ, but these are stated for smooth tables, and 39.5 assumes only continuity. The proof's remark that smoothness was used only for tested ⇒ pointwise is right for the descent, injectivity and composition. However, the smoothness clauses of those conclusions ("Smoothness … pass to these correlations"; "Multilinearity, symmetry and smoothness follow") then hold with "continuous" unless `ex-phase-caps` is invoked. Suggest one clause, e.g. "with smooth replaced by continuous, or smooth under the hypotheses of Proposition 37.1".

**O1 (optional): one name for the subpower norm.** The relay states its bound in "actual inverse terminal coordinates" |X_{N,p}^{-1}v|_1. `ope-filtration.tex` calls this the "bare" norm, and `finite-rank-kernel-transport.tex` the "inverse-calibrated" norm. The canonical text identifies them only implicitly: ope-filtration l.290–291 equates bare with inverse-calibrated, and the relay's final paragraph names its labels as the comparison inputs of the filtration. A parenthetical at l.403 would let the reader match the three names without searching.

**O2 (optional): different refinements per basis label.** "Its one compact refinement is made for this row" is right. A reader may still ask about the different refinements for different (d, e, j). One sentence would settle it: f_θ and M_θ are limits along the original ordered filter, so each b_{de,j} satisfies an identity between refinement-independent quantities, and nothing has to be extracted jointly. `lifts_model` likewise calls `brace` separately for each basis vector.

**O3 (cosmetic).** `Paragraph~\ref{par:phase-inclusion-attachment}` (l.538) renders as "Paragraph 39.2", the subsection number, because a `\paragraph` label takes the enclosing counter. Consider "the first paragraph of Section~\ref{subsec:phase-inclusion-inheritance}".
